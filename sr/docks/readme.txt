Welcome to Stride Rush!
In this audio game, you ride a belt that carries you forward one step at a time, and you have to jump over, duck under, switch off, open or smash through everything that gets in your way.

How to play.

Once a run starts, you never stop moving. The belt carries you to the right, and you'll hear a footstep with every step you take. The ground under your feet changes every 10 to 20 seconds, so listen for new footstep sounds as you go.
You start at a speed of 500 milliseconds per step. Every 10 seconds you get a little faster, 10 milliseconds at a time, until you reach the top speed of 50 milliseconds per step.
Obstacles are heard ahead of you, about 3 seconds before you reach them. Jump at the right moment to sail over most of them. If you're still on the ground when you step onto one, it will catch you. There are four exceptions: you crouch under projectiles, you switch off force fields with one of your hands, you break walls with your weapons, and you open doors with your hands.
A jump lasts about a second. You can't jump again until you land, and when you land you'll hear the ground you came down on.
Runs are endless. The goal is to get as far as you can before something stops you.

Obstacles.

Hazards, such as pits, chasms, fire, lava, water, quicksand and electricity, end your run straight away if you fall into one. Each hazard has a five-step ledge leading up to it, and as you step along the ledge you'll hear a five-cue countdown, one cue per step, with the last cue on the step right before the hazard. At slower speeds, wait for the last few cues before you jump. Once you're fast enough, jumping on any ledge step will carry you over.
Spikes swing back and forth with their own rhythm, which you'll hear as you approach them. Getting hit by spikes costs you 1 life.
Mines make a sound when they appear, then light up and start ticking once you get close. Stepping onto a mine costs you 2 lives. Once you jump over a mine, you'll hear it keep ticking as it fades out behind you.
Force fields hum ahead of you while they're on, and jumping or crouching won't get you past one. Press either hand key during a force field's countdown to switch it off. You'll hear the switch, then the field's sound changes as it shuts off, and you can run straight through. At the fastest speeds, where the countdown is very short, you can still switch one off in the last half second before you reach it. If you reach a force field while it's still on, it costs you 1 life and throws you 5 steps forward past it.
Projectiles, such as missiles, cars, boulders and firewalls, fly toward you at head height, twice as fast as you run, so you'll hear them rushing in. Hold the crouch key so you're crouched when one reaches you, and it flies over you and fades away behind. A crouch only lasts a second and a half, so at slower speeds wait for the last few countdown cues before you crouch. Jumping won't help, and if one hits you, it costs you 1 life.
Walls are glass windows standing in your way, and you'll hear them ahead of you as you get closer. Jumping or crouching won't get you past one, so swing or fire your weapon at it as it comes. Your weapons reach about 2 seconds ahead of you, so start early. Each hit cracks it a little more, and once it shatters you can run straight through. Walls get tougher the faster you run: early walls break with a couple of swings, while the fastest ones take a strong weapon and quick hands. If you crash into a wall that's still standing, it costs you 1 life.
Doors mark your progress. Each time you get another 50 milliseconds faster, a real door comes up ahead of you. Real doors are locked, so you'll need the key that turns up earlier in each stretch. Once the door is within about 2 seconds of you, choose a key in your inventory and press U to unlock it, and you'll hear the lock give. Then press either hand key in the last few steps before the door to open it, and the door carries you through into the next stretch. If you try to open a door that's still locked, you'll hear it refuse. While it moves you, your footsteps go quiet and you can't jump, crouch, use your weapons or use your inventory, until it closes behind you. Once you reach the top speed, there are no more real doors.
Watch out for fake doors, though, which can turn up at any time. They sound slightly higher or lower than a real door, and they won't open: your hands or a key just find them jammed, and keys aren't used up on them. Break them with your weapons the same way as walls, and just as tough at your speed. Doors have no countdown, so listen for them as they come. Crashing into any door, real or fake, costs you 3 lives.
Spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles and walls have the same five-cue countdown as hazards, at every speed, but without a ledge. A wall's countdown plays two notes at once, and once a wall is broken or a force field switched off, its countdown stops. Each kind of obstacle has its own countdown sounds, so you can tell which one is coming from the countdown alone.

Lives.

You start each run with 1 life, so for now, any obstacle that catches you ends the run. Spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles, walls and doors cost lives, and if you have lives to spare you'll hear a sound and keep running. When your last life is gone, the run ends and you return to the main menu. While you wear a steel shield, it takes those hits instead, so you keep your lives.

Crouching.

You can crouch by holding down the crouch key. Your footsteps go quiet while you're crouched, and you stand back up when you let go, or on your own after a second and a half. After standing up, you have to wait half a second before you can crouch again. Pressing the jump key while crouched jumps straight away. Crouching is how you get under projectiles.

Weapons.

You have ten weapons, one on each number key from 1 to 0: the bat, boot, club, feat, fist, hammer, knife, laser, machine gun and pistol. Each run starts with the bat in your hands, and each weapon is stronger than the one before it. Press the space bar to swing a melee weapon or fire a gun. The machine gun keeps firing for as long as you hold the space bar. A melee weapon hits straight away, while a gun fires a bullet that you'll hear flying off ahead of you until it lands. A bullet only flies as far as your weapons reach, so a shot fired at something still too far away falls short and wastes the round.
The machine gun and pistol use ammo. Each starts every run with 50 rounds loaded and 50 in reserve, and they keep their own ammo separately. A reserve can hold up to 200 rounds. Once a gun is empty you'll hear it click instead of firing. You can reload any time a gun isn't full, and you'll hear a ping when it's ready. When the reserve runs out, that gun is empty until you open an ammo pack.
Your weapons are for breaking walls and fake doors, and they all reach the same distance ahead of you. Real doors can't be harmed. The stronger the weapon, the fewer hits a wall takes. Your weapons always hit the nearest thing ahead of you, and that includes any item lying ahead of you, such as an ammo pack, key or shield, which breaks if you hit it.

Items and your inventory.

Every so often an ammo pack turns up ahead of you instead of an obstacle, and you'll hear it waiting there. Press either hand key in the last few steps before you reach it to pick it up, and it goes into your inventory. Be careful: if you run onto a pack, or land on it from a jump, it breaks and is lost, and so does shooting or swinging at it. A jump that carries you right over it just misses it.
Packs stay in your inventory until you open them. Opening one adds 5 to 10 rounds to the reserve of the gun you're holding, so draw the gun you want to fill first. If you're holding a melee weapon, or that gun's reserve is already full, the pack stays in your inventory for later. Your inventory starts empty every run.
Keys turn up once in every stretch, somewhere before its real door, and you pick them up and lose them the same way as ammo packs. They come in several colors, but the color doesn't matter: any key unlocks any real door. Each key you use is gone, and a key you don't use stays in your inventory for a later door. If there's no door within reach, the door ahead is already unlocked, or it's a fake door, the key stays in your inventory.
Steel shields turn up now and then, a little less often than ammo packs, and you pick them up and lose them the same way. Choose one in your inventory and press U to wear it like armor. You can only wear one at a time, so any others wait in your inventory until the one you're wearing breaks. A shield takes the damage from spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles, walls and doors in place of your lives. You'll hear the obstacle hit your shield instead of you, and a force field still throws you forward. A fresh shield takes about 10 hits from spikes, projectiles, force fields or walls, 5 mines or 4 door crashes, and the hit that wears it out is still fully blocked, so you'll hear it break and lose nothing. Shields can't save you from hazards. Press M to hear how strong your shield is.
Life potions turn up only rarely, and you pick them up and lose them the same way as ammo packs. Drink one with U and you get 1 extra life, and there's no limit to how many lives you can build up.
Player teleporters are a gamble. Pick one up the same way, then press U, and it throws you a random 2 to 5 seconds' worth of belt either forward or back. It never drops you right on top of an obstacle, but it can drop you just in front of one, so be ready to react the moment you land. If it sends you back, anything you passed that's still waiting there, such as a spike or a hazard, comes up again, while anything you already dealt with, such as a broken wall or an opened door, stays that way.

Keyboard commands

Up arrow or letter W: Jumps, if pressed.
Down arrow or letter S: Crouches, to duck under projectiles, if held down.
Tab or letter A: Uses your left hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item, if pressed.
Enter or letter D: Uses your right hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item, if pressed.
Letter I: Moves to the next item in your inventory, if pressed.
Shift plus letter I: Moves to the previous item in your inventory, if pressed.
Letter U: Uses the item you're on in your inventory, such as opening an ammo pack, unlocking a door with a key, putting on a shield, drinking a life potion or using a teleporter, if pressed.
Numbers 1 through 0: Draws the bat, boot, club, feat, fist, hammer, knife, laser, machine gun or pistol, if pressed.
Space: Swings or fires your weapon, if pressed. With the machine gun, it keeps firing, if held down.
Letter R: Reloads your gun, if pressed.
Letter X: Announces how much ammo your gun has loaded and in reserve, if pressed.
Letter H: Announces how many lives you have, if pressed.
Letter M: Announces how strong the shield you're wearing is, as a percentage, if pressed.
Letter Z: Announces how far you've run, in tiles, if pressed.
Letter C: Announces your current speed, in milliseconds per step, if pressed.
Letter T: Announces how long you've been running for, if pressed.
Page up: Raises the in-game music volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Page down: Lowers the in-game music volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Home: Raises the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
End: Lowers the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Escape or letter P: Brings up the pause menu, if pressed. The pause menu lets you resume the game, or quit back to the main menu. Pressing escape in the pause menu also resumes the game.
Alt plus F4: Exits the game right away during a run, if pressed.

Speech buffer commands

Everything the game speaks during a run is kept in speech buffers, so you can go back and review it.
Comma: Moves to the previous message in the current buffer, if pressed/held down.
Period: Moves to the next message in the current buffer, if pressed/held down.
Shift plus comma: Moves to the first message in the current buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus period: Moves to the last message in the current buffer, if pressed.
Left bracket: Moves to the previous buffer, if pressed/held down.
Right bracket: Moves to the next buffer, if pressed/held down.
Shift plus left bracket: Moves to the first buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus right bracket: Moves to the last buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus letter M: Mutes or unmutes the current buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus letter C: Copies the current message to the clipboard, if pressed.
Alt plus shift plus letter C: Opens the current message in a box you can read with the arrow keys, if pressed.
Shift plus letter B: Brings up the buffer menu, where you can copy, export, mute or review a buffer or a message, if pressed.
Shift plus backslash: Exports the current buffer to a text file, then clears it, if pressed.
