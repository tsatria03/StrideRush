Welcome to Stride Rush!
In this audio game, you ride a belt that carries you forward one step at a time, and you have to jump over, duck under or switch off everything that gets in your way.

How to play.

Once a run starts, you never stop moving. The belt carries you to the right, and you'll hear a footstep with every step you take. The ground under your feet changes every so often, so listen for new footstep sounds as you go.
You start at a speed of 500 milliseconds per step. Every 10 seconds you get a little faster, 10 milliseconds at a time, until you reach the top speed of 50 milliseconds per step.
Obstacles are heard ahead of you, about 3 seconds before you reach them. Jump at the right moment to sail over most of them. If you're still on the ground when you step onto one, it will catch you. There are two exceptions: you crouch under projectiles, and you switch off force fields with one of your hands.
A jump lasts about a second. You can't jump again until you land, and when you land you'll hear the ground you came down on.
Runs are endless. The goal is to get as far as you can before something stops you.

Obstacles.

Hazards, such as pits, chasms, fire, lava, water, quicksand and electricity, end your run straight away if you fall into one. Each hazard has a ledge leading up to it, and as you step along the ledge you'll hear a countdown, one cue per step, with the last cue on the step right before the hazard. At slower speeds the ledge is longer, so wait for the last few cues before you jump. Once you're fast enough, jumping on any ledge step will carry you over.
Spikes swing back and forth with their own rhythm, which you'll hear as you approach them. Getting hit by spikes costs you 1 life.
Mines make a sound when they appear, then light up and start ticking once you get close. Stepping onto a mine costs you 2 lives. Once you jump over a mine, you'll hear it keep ticking as it fades out behind you.
Force fields hum ahead of you while they're on, and jumping or crouching won't get you past one. Press either hand key during a force field's countdown to switch it off. You'll hear the switch from the side of the hand you used, then the field's sound changes as it shuts off, and you can run straight through. At the fastest speeds, where the countdown is very short, you can still switch one off in the last half second before you reach it. If you reach a force field while it's still on, it costs you 1 life and throws you 5 steps forward past it.
Projectiles, such as missiles, cars, boulders and firewalls, fly toward you at head height, twice as fast as you run, so you'll hear them rushing in. Hold the crouch key so you're crouched when one reaches you, and it flies over you and fades away behind. A crouch only lasts a second and a half, so at slower speeds wait for the last few countdown cues before you crouch. Jumping won't help, and if one hits you, it costs you 1 life.
Spikes, mines, force fields and projectiles have a countdown too, just like hazards, timed the same way, but without a ledge. Each kind of obstacle has its own countdown sounds, so you can tell which one is coming from the countdown alone.

Lives.

You start each run with 1 life, so for now, any obstacle that catches you ends the run. Spikes, mines, force fields and projectiles cost lives, and if you have lives to spare you'll hear a sound and keep running. When your last life is gone, the run ends and you return to the main menu.

Crouching.

You can crouch by holding down the crouch key. Your footsteps go quiet while you're crouched, and you stand back up when you let go, or on your own after a second and a half. After standing up, you have to wait about a second before you can crouch again. Pressing the jump key while crouched jumps straight away. Crouching is how you get under projectiles.

Keyboard commands

Up arrow or letter W: Jumps, if pressed.
Down arrow or letter S: Crouches, to duck under projectiles, if held down.
Tab or letter A: Uses your left hand, to switch off a force field, if pressed.
Enter or letter D: Uses your right hand, to switch off a force field, if pressed.
Letter L: Announces how many lives you have, if pressed.
Letter Z: Announces how far you've run, in tiles, if pressed.
Letter C: Announces your current speed, in milliseconds per step, if pressed.
Letter T: Announces how long you've been running for, if pressed.
Page up: Raises the in-game music volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Page down: Lowers the in-game music volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Home: Raises the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
End: Lowers the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Escape or letter P: Brings up the pause menu, if pressed. The pause menu lets you resume the game, or quit back to the main menu. Pressing escape in the pause menu also resumes the game.
Alt plus F4: Exits the game right away during a run, if pressed.

Speech buffer commands

Everything the game speaks during a run is kept in speech buffers, so you can go back and review it.
Comma: Moves to the previous message in the current buffer, if pressed/held down.
Period: Moves to the next message in the current buffer, if pressed/held down.
Shift plus comma: Moves to the first message in the current buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus period: Moves to the last message in the current buffer, if pressed.
Left bracket: Moves to the previous buffer, if pressed/held down.
Right bracket: Moves to the next buffer, if pressed/held down.
Shift plus left bracket: Moves to the first buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus right bracket: Moves to the last buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus letter M: Mutes or unmutes the current buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus letter C: Copies the current message to the clipboard, if pressed.
Alt plus shift plus letter C: Opens the current message in a box you can read with the arrow keys, if pressed.
Shift plus letter B: Brings up the buffer menu, where you can copy, export, mute or review a buffer or a message, if pressed.
Shift plus backslash: Exports the current buffer to a text file, then clears it, if pressed.
