Welcome to Stride Rush!
In this audio game, you ride a belt that carries you forward one step at a time, and you have to jump over, duck under, dodge, switch off, open or smash through everything that gets in your way.

Getting started.

From the main menu, choose game menu, then play game, then practice run, and pick hazards, the only obstacle open at first, where nothing can end your run. Once you've jumped over 5 hazards, endless run opens, so go back to play game, choose endless run, and pick easy, the only difficulty open at first. Learn sounds, in the game menu, lets you hear every obstacle's countdown before you meet it in a run.
The belt carries you forward on its own, so you never press a key to move ahead. Listen in front of you: every obstacle plays a five-cue countdown as it comes, and on easy you have time to act on the last few cues.
Press space, the up arrow or W to jump over hazards, spikes and mines, and hold the down arrow or S to crouch under projectiles.
Press the left and right arrows, or Q and E, to switch lanes out of a barrier's way.
Press A for your left hand and D for your right, to switch off force fields, pick up items and open doors, and press both together to clap animals away.
Press control or F to swing or fire your weapon at fake walls and fake doors, and the number keys to choose a different weapon.
Press shift plus tab and tab, or I and O, to move through your inventory, and enter or U to use an item. Before each real door, pick up its key, then use it to unlock the door.
In an endless run, open gold packs for gold coins, press Y to hear how many you have, and press slash or F8 to spend them in the player shop.
Press P to pause, or escape to quit the run.
You can also play with a joystick or game controller, in every menu and in a run.

The keyboard and joystick document, under game in the documentation menu, lists every key and button, including the ones you can pick up later, such as the readouts and the volume keys. Every obstacle, item, mode and feature is explained in detail in its own document there too.

How to play.

Once a run starts, you never stop moving. The belt carries you straight ahead, one footstep at a time, and gets faster as you go. Obstacles are heard in front of you, about 3 seconds before you reach them, getting louder as they come closer, and each one plays a five-cue countdown so you know when to act.
Most obstacles are jumped over, but some need something else: crouching, switching lanes, your hands or your weapons. Items turn up along the way for your inventory, and powerups give you a good or bad effect for a while. Runs are endless, so the goal is to get as far as you can before something stops you.
Each obstacle, item and mode has its own document, under game in the documentation menu, explaining how it works and how to get past it. A good place to start is movement, then health and lives, then each obstacle as it joins your runs.

Menus.

The main menu has eight options. The documentation menu has two sections: game, with a document for each part of the game, from every obstacle and item to the game modes, and main, with the changelog, credits, readme, todo list and translations. Each opens in a window you can move through with the arrow keys and close when you're done, and each list remembers where you were.
The game menu is where you start a run, by choosing play game, learn the game's sounds, view your game achievements or statistics, or clear, copy or export all of your game progress, as the learning sounds, achievements and statistics documents explain. Play game asks which game you'd like to play: endless run, which then asks for a difficulty and opens once you've practiced hazards, timed run, which asks for a difficulty too and opens once medium does in endless run, practice run, which asks for an obstacle and a speed, or free run, which asks for a speed and an environment and opens once insane does in endless run, each explained in its own document under game in the documentation menu.
The settings menu, change game version and check for updates are described below too. Test speakers plays a sound so you can check your speakers or headphones, and you can press enter to end it early. Restart game closes Stride Rush and opens it again, and exit game closes it. Pressing escape on the main menu also exits.
During a run, press P for the pause menu, where you can resume, restart or quit the run. View session statuses lists where you stand right now: your health, lives, shield, lane, environment, speed, distance, time and heart rate, in any kind of run. Apart from your lane, which only it tells you, these are the same things the status keys tell you. In endless and free runs, it also lists your attack, defence, max health, max shield strength and shield defence.
Restarting or quitting asks you first, since you'll lose the run you're on. In an endless run, the pause menu also has view session results, which lists your stats for the run so far, the same way session statistics does, and options to copy them to your clipboard or export them to a file, as the statistics document explains. The run stays paused while you look. Pressing escape during a run asks straight away whether you want to quit, and the run stops moving while you decide. Both the pause menu and the quit question pause every sound but the ambience, so the game is never silent while you decide. Whenever you leave a run this way, its sounds fade out.
When an endless run ends in a death, your results for the run come up straight away, starting with the difficulty and your death outcome, which tells you what killed you. Pressing escape or back then asks what you'd like to do now: copy or export your results, replay the run on the same difficulty, or return to the game menu. Copying or exporting brings you back to the same question, so you can do both before you choose. The game tab of the settings menu can turn off the results, so the question comes straight after you die, starting with "You have died", and can leave copy and export out of the question.

The game's folders.

The game keeps its files in three folders, each split into a main part, for the game as a whole, and a game part, for what you meet in a run.
The data folder holds your settings files in its config folder: data/config/main has keyboard.ini and joystick.ini, your key and joystick bindings, and data/config/game has the achievements. Its lang folder holds the game's languages, which the translations document explains.
The docks folder holds these documents in a folder for each language, such as docks/lang/english: its main folder has the readme, changelog, todo list, credits and translations, and its game folder has a document for each part of the game.
The sounds folder holds every sound: sounds/main has the game's own events, your own sounds and the menus, and sounds/game has the environments, weapons and items. The customizing sounds document explains how to change them or add your own.

Updates.

Stride Rush can update itself. If you toggle the setting in the general tab of the settings menu, it checks quietly for a new version each time it starts, and only speaks up if there is one. Choose check for updates in the main menu to check at any time, and it tells you if you already have the latest version or if it can't check right now.
When there's a new version, you're asked whether to download it, and then whether you want the Windows installer or the portable version. While it downloads, you'll hear beeps that rise as it goes. Press space for the percentage, 1, 2 and 3 for the file size, how much has downloaded and how much is left, m to mute or unmute the beeps, h to hear these keys again, or escape to cancel. Afterwards, run installer starts the installer and closes the game, so the new version replaces this one. Extract file unpacks the portable version into an sdr folder inside your game folder, then closes the game, so you can run the new version from there.
Change game version, in the main menu, lets you type a different version number for the updater to compare against, using numbers and periods only. Type an older one and check for updates offers you the latest version again. It lasts until you close the game.

Settings.

The settings menu is a window with a list of tabs at the top. Move through the tabs with the arrow keys, and tab through the options on each one. Control plus tab and control plus shift plus tab move to the next or previous tab from anywhere in the window, wrapping around at either end. Nothing you tick or slide takes effect until you choose save settings, and cancel or escape leaves everything as it was. Each volume slider goes from 0 to 100 percent in steps of 1, where 100 percent is full volume, and the volume keys and the sliders always agree, since each shows what the other last set. The menu music, music and ambience start at 80 percent, and the footsteps, heartbeat and master volumes at 100 percent.
General: choose whether the game checks for updates when it starts, which is off by default. Reset settings puts every setting back to how it started, after asking you first, then restarts the game. Your key and joystick bindings aren't touched.
Game: set the volume of the game's ambience, your footsteps, your heartbeat and the music, then the health format and shield format, which choose whether H, M, the session statuses and a health potion speak your health and shield as a percentage, which is how they start, an amount, or both. Next, choose whether things behind you sound a little lower in pitch, which is on by default and helps you tell what's behind you from what's ahead, and whether escape asks before quitting a run. If you switch off the escape question, escape leaves the run straight away. Last, choose whether your session results come up after you die, whether the menu that follows offers to copy or export them, and whether a practice death offers its retry options, all toggled on by default.
Joystick: choose whether your joystick vibrates when you're hit, which is on by default, then rebind your joystick's buttons, or view every joystick binding, by button name. The rebind list works like the keyboard's below: select an action, then add another button to it, change its button, or remove its buttons, and each change is saved as soon as you make it. When asked, press any button on your joystick; pressing escape, or nothing for 10 seconds, cancels. You're then asked whether to change its trigger from press to down or release, except for fire and duck, which handle their own. Two actions can share a button when their triggers differ, but fire and duck never share one, and if the button clashes with another action, you're asked whether to replace it.
Keyboard: rebind your keys, or view every key binding. In the rebind list, select an action, then add another key to it, change its key, or remove its keys, and each change is saved as soon as you make it. A key can be a combination: hold any shift, control or alt keys first, then press the other key last. Holding one side, such as the left shift, binds that side only, while holding both shifts, both controls or both alts binds either side. A control key can also be bound on its own, by pressing and releasing it with nothing else. Some keyboards can't send both sides of a modifier together with every key, so if nothing happens, try one side or a different key.
You're then asked whether to change the key's trigger from press to repeat, down or release, except for fire and duck, which handle their own. Two actions can share a key when their triggers differ, but fire and duck never share one, and if the key clashes with another action, you're asked whether to replace it, which takes it off only that action. An action left with no keys stays in the list, so you can give it one later.
Menu: turn on wrapping, first letter navigation, position information and side scrolling in menus, and choose whether the learn sounds menu tells you when a sound has several versions, which is on by default.
Sound: set the menu music volume and the master game volume, which turns every sound in the game up or down together. Turn HRTF on or off, which is on by default and makes sounds in front of you, behind you and to your sides easier to place through headphones. You can also choose between fading out by volume, which is on by default, or by pitch when you leave a run, choose whether the sounds in the learn sounds menu keep looping until you move to another, which is also on by default, choose whether the game goes silent while you're in another window, which is off by default, and pick which sound device the game plays through. Your device choice is saved straight away.
Speech: choose whether the game stops speaking while you're in another window, which is off by default, while your speech buffers still keep everything it would have said, then set up your voice, either your screen reader or another voice with its own rate, pitch and volume, and preview how it sounds.
During a run, F2 opens the settings with only the game, menu and speech tabs, and F1 shows your key bindings, then your joystick bindings while a joystick is connected. The run is paused while either is open, and carries on when you close it.
Your settings, including your music, ambience and menu music volumes, are remembered from one launch to the next.

Game conclusion.

Stride Rush started as a simple audio runner, a belt and a footstep on every step, and has grown into a game of environments, dozens of obstacles and items, five difficulty levels, timed runs, practice runs and a free run sandbox, with joystick support and sounds you can make your own.
Every obstacle, item, mode and feature has its own document under game in the documentation menu, so whenever something new turns up in your runs, you can find out how it works there.

Whether you're jumping your first hazards or chasing a faster run on chaos, we hope this document gives you everything you need to keep up your stride.

Thanks for playing, and happy sprinting!
