Welcome to Stride Rush!
In this audio game, you ride a belt that carries you forward one step at a time, and you have to jump over, duck under, dodge, switch off, open or smash through everything that gets in your way.

Getting started.

From the main menu, choose game menu, then play game, then practice run, and pick hazards, the only obstacle open at first, where nothing can end your run. Once you've jumped over 5 hazards, endless run opens, so go back to play game, choose endless run, and pick easy, the only difficulty open at first. Learn sounds, in the game menu, lets you hear every obstacle's countdown before you meet it in a run.
The belt carries you forward on its own, so you never press a key to move ahead. Listen in front of you: every obstacle plays a five-cue countdown as it comes, and on easy you have time to act on the last few cues.
Press the up arrow or W to jump over hazards, spikes and mines, and hold the down arrow or S to crouch under projectiles.
Press the left and right arrows, or Q and E, to switch lanes out of a barrier's way.
Press A for your left hand and D for your right, to switch off force fields, pick up items and open doors, and press both together to clap animals away.
Press space to swing or fire your weapon at fake walls and fake doors, and the number keys to choose a different weapon.
Press shift plus tab and tab, or I and O, to move through your inventory, and enter or U to use an item. Before each real door, pick up its key, then use it to unlock the door.
Press P to pause, or escape to quit the run.
You can also play with a joystick or game controller, in every menu and in a run, as the joystick commands below describe.

The keyboard commands just below list every key, including the ones you can pick up later, such as the readouts and the volume keys. Every obstacle, item, mode and feature is explained in detail in its own document, under game in the documentation menu.

Keyboard commands

Up arrow or letter W: Jumps.
Down arrow or letter S: Crouches, to duck under projectiles and to fight insects.
Left arrow or letter Q: Moves one lane to the left.
Right arrow or letter E: Moves one lane to the right.
Letter A: Uses your left hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Letter D: Uses your right hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Both hand keys together, A and D: Claps, to scare away an animal. With nothing for your hands to do, a single hand makes a whoosh and a clap is just a clap.
Shift plus tab or letter I: Moves to the previous item in your inventory.
Tab or letter O: Moves to the next item in your inventory.
Enter or letter U: Uses the item you're on in your inventory, such as opening an ammo pack, unlocking a door with a key, putting on a shield, using a life capsule, a health potion or a teleporter.
Numbers 1 through 0: Draws the bat, boot, club, dagger, fist, hammer, knife, sword, machine gun or pistol.
Space: Swings or fires your weapon. With the machine gun, it keeps firing.
Letter R: Reloads your gun.
Letter X: Announces how much ammo your gun has loaded and in reserve.
Letter H: Announces how much health you have left, and how many lives you have, as a percentage, an amount such as 350 of 500, or both, whichever you choose in the game tab of the settings menu.
Letter M: Announces how strong the shield you're wearing is, as a percentage, an amount such as 320 of 400 endurance, or both, whichever you choose in the game tab of the settings menu.
Letter L: Announces which lane you're in.
Letter Z: Announces how far you've run, in steps.
Letter C: Announces your current speed, in steps a second.
Letter T: Announces how long you've been running for, or in a timed run, how much time is left on the clock and how long until you reach the finish line.
Letter B: Announces your heart rate, in beats per minute.
Letter V: Announces which environment you're in.
Page up or F4: Raises the in-game music volume by 1 decibel.
Page down or F3: Lowers the in-game music volume by 1 decibel.
Home or F6: Raises the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel.
End or F5: Lowers the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel.
Letter P: Brings up the pause menu. The pause menu lets you resume, restart or quit the run, view your session statuses, and in an endless run, view, copy or export your session results. Pressing escape in the pause menu also resumes the run.
Escape: Asks whether you want to quit the run, or quits it straight away if you've turned the question off in the settings menu.
F1: Shows every key binding, and every joystick binding while a joystick is connected, with the run paused until you close it.
F2: Opens the settings menu with its game, menu and speech tabs, with the run paused until you close it.
Backspace or F7: In a free run, opens the free run console, with the run paused until you close it.

Joystick commands

Stride Rush works with most game controllers, such as Xbox, PlayStation and Switch Pro controllers. Plug one in before or after starting the game: plugging one in or unplugging it is announced once, and unplugging it during a run pauses the run. In a run, these are the buttons to start with, and you can change them in the joystick tab of the settings menu. The left stick always works the same as the d-pad. If your controller can vibrate, it gives a light tap when your shield takes a blow, a rumble when you're hurt, a stronger one when you lose a life, and the strongest when you die, so the harder it shakes, the worse the blow.

D-pad up: Jumps.
D-pad down: Crouches, to duck under projectiles and to fight insects.
D-pad left: Moves one lane to the left.
D-pad right: Moves one lane to the right.
Left shoulder: Uses your left hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Right shoulder: Uses your right hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Both shoulders together: Claps, to scare away an animal.
Left stick click: Moves to the previous item in your inventory.
Right stick click: Moves to the next item in your inventory.
B button: Uses the item you're on in your inventory.
A button: Swings or fires your weapon. With the machine gun, it keeps firing.
X button: Reloads your gun.
Y button: Announces how much ammo your gun has loaded and in reserve.
Start: Brings up the pause menu.
Back: Asks whether you want to quit the run, or quits it straight away if you've turned the question off in the settings menu.

Choosing a weapon, the readouts, the volume, F1, F2, the free run console and the speech buffers have no button to start with, but you can give any of them one.
In every menu and dialog, the buttons are always these, and can't be changed. Text boxes still need the keyboard.

D-pad or left stick: Moves, like the arrow keys. Hold it to keep moving.
A button: Chooses, like enter.
B button: Goes back, like escape.
Right shoulder: Moves to the next control, like tab.
Left shoulder: Moves to the previous control, like shift plus tab.
Y button: In a message that says to press enter to continue, copies the message, like the letter C.

Your joystick's buttons are kept in joystick.ini, in the data/main folder, which you can also edit by hand. Each line is an action, an equals sign, then its buttons by name, separated by commas, such as jump=DPAD_UP, the same way keyboard.ini names its keys. The names are A, B, X and Y for the four face buttons, BACK, GUIDE and START, LEFT_STICK and RIGHT_STICK for the stick clicks, LEFT_SHOULDER and RIGHT_SHOULDER, and DPAD_UP, DPAD_DOWN, DPAD_LEFT and DPAD_RIGHT, in capitals or not; any other button your controller has, such as a touchpad or a mute button, is BUTTON_ followed by its number, such as BUTTON_15. A button can end with :down, :release or :up, such as duck_held=DPAD_DOWN:down, and with none, it's a press. A file with the old numbers instead of names still works. If joystick.ini is missing or can't be read, the game tells you when it starts, then runs with the keyboard only.

Speech buffer commands

Everything the game speaks during a run is kept in speech buffers, so you can go back and review it. The all buffer holds every message, and the rest each hold one kind: achievements, for the achievements you earn; doors, for locked, jammed and unlocked doors; items, for what you pick up, your inventory and what your items do; powerups, for the powerups you collect and when they wear off; practice, for the hints in a practice run; status, for your health, shield, lane, distance, speed and running time; weapons, for drawing, reloading and your ammo; and misc, for your music and ambience volume, the environments you enter, and anything you unlock in a practice run. A buffer only shows up once something has been spoken into it, and they come in alphabetical order after all.

Comma: Moves to the previous message in the current buffer.
Period: Moves to the next message in the current buffer.
Shift plus comma: Moves to the first message in the current buffer.
Shift plus period: Moves to the last message in the current buffer.
Left bracket: Moves to the previous buffer.
Right bracket: Moves to the next buffer.
Shift plus left bracket: Moves to the first buffer.
Shift plus right bracket: Moves to the last buffer.
Shift plus letter M: Mutes or unmutes the current buffer. A muted buffer stays muted the next time you play, until you unmute it.
Shift plus letter C: Copies the current message to the clipboard.
Alt plus shift plus letter C: Opens the current message in a box you can read with the arrow keys.
Shift plus letter B: Brings up the buffer menu, where you can copy, export, mute or review a buffer or a message.
Shift plus backslash: Exports the current buffer to a text file, then clears it.

How to play.

Once a run starts, you never stop moving. The belt carries you straight ahead, one footstep at a time, and gets faster as you go. Obstacles are heard in front of you, about 3 seconds before you reach them, getting louder as they come closer, and each one plays a five-cue countdown so you know when to act.
Most obstacles are jumped over, but some need something else: crouching, switching lanes, your hands or your weapons. Items turn up along the way for your inventory, and powerups give you a good or bad effect for a while. Runs are endless, so the goal is to get as far as you can before something stops you.
Each obstacle, item and mode has its own document, under game in the documentation menu, explaining how it works and how to get past it. A good place to start is movement, then health and lives, then each obstacle as it joins your runs.

Menus.

The main menu has eight options. The documentation menu has two sections: game, with a document for each part of the game, from every obstacle and item to the game modes, and main, with the changelog, credits, readme and todo list. Each opens in a window you can move through with the arrow keys and close when you're done, and each list remembers where you were.
The game menu is where you start a run, by choosing play game, learn the game's sounds, view your game achievements or statistics, or clear, copy or export all of your game progress, as the learning sounds, achievements and statistics documents explain. Play game asks which game you'd like to play: endless run, which then asks for a difficulty and opens once you've practiced hazards, timed run, which asks for a difficulty too and opens once medium does in endless run, practice run, which asks for an obstacle and a speed, or free run, which asks for a speed and an environment and opens once insane does in endless run, each explained in its own document under game in the documentation menu.
The settings menu, change game version and check for updates are described below too. Test speakers plays a sound so you can check your speakers or headphones, and you can press enter to end it early. Restart game closes Stride Rush and opens it again, and exit game closes it. Pressing escape on the main menu also exits.
During a run, press P for the pause menu, where you can resume, restart or quit the run. View session statuses lists where you stand right now, the same things the status keys tell you: your health and lives, shield, lane, environment, speed, distance, time and heart rate, in any kind of run. Restarting or quitting asks you first, since you'll lose the run you're on. In an endless run, the pause menu also has view session results, which lists your stats for the run so far, the same way session statistics does, and options to copy them to your clipboard or export them to a file, as the statistics document explains. The run stays paused while you look. Pressing escape during a run asks straight away whether you want to quit, and the run stops moving while you decide. Both the pause menu and the quit question pause every sound but the ambience, so the game is never silent while you decide. Whenever you leave a run this way, its sounds fade out.
When an endless run ends in a death, your results for the run come up straight away, starting with the difficulty and your death outcome, which tells you what killed you. Pressing escape or back then asks what you'd like to do now: copy or export your results, replay the run on the same difficulty, or return to the game menu. Copying or exporting brings you back to the same question, so you can do both before you choose. The game tab of the settings menu can turn off the results, so the question comes straight after you die, starting with "You have died", and can leave copy and export out of the question.

The game's folders.

The game keeps its files in three folders, each split into a main part, for the game as a whole, and a game part, for what you meet in a run.
The data folder holds your settings files: data/main has keyboard.ini and joystick.ini, your key and joystick bindings, and data/game has the achievements.
The docks folder holds these documents: docks/main has the readme, changelog, todo list and credits, and docks/game has a document for each part of the game.
The sounds folder holds every sound: sounds/main has the game's own events, your own sounds and the menus, and sounds/game has the environments, weapons and items. The customizing sounds document explains how to change them or add your own.

Updates.

Stride Rush can update itself. If you toggle the setting in the general tab of the settings menu, it checks quietly for a new version each time it starts, and only speaks up if there is one. Choose check for updates in the main menu to check at any time, and it tells you if you already have the latest version or if it can't check right now.
When there's a new version, you're asked whether to download it, and then whether you want the Windows installer or the portable version. While it downloads, you'll hear beeps that rise as it goes. Press space for the percentage, 1, 2 and 3 for the file size, how much has downloaded and how much is left, m to mute or unmute the beeps, h to hear these keys again, or escape to cancel. Afterwards, run installer starts the installer and closes the game, so the new version replaces this one. Extract file unpacks the portable version into an sdr folder inside your game folder, then closes the game, so you can run the new version from there.
Change game version, in the main menu, lets you type a different version number for the updater to compare against, using numbers and periods only. Type an older one and check for updates offers you the latest version again. It lasts until you close the game.

Settings.

The settings menu is a window with a list of tabs at the top. Move through the tabs with the arrow keys, and tab through the options on each one. Control plus tab and control plus shift plus tab move to the next or previous tab from anywhere in the window, wrapping around at either end. Nothing you tick or slide takes effect until you choose save settings, and cancel or escape leaves everything as it was. Each volume slider goes from 0 to 100 percent in steps of 1, where 100 percent is full volume, and the volume keys and the sliders always agree, since each shows what the other last set. The menu music, music and ambience start at 80 percent, and the footsteps, heartbeat and master volumes at 100 percent.
General: choose whether the game checks for updates when it starts, which is off by default. Reset settings puts every setting back to how it started, after asking you first, then restarts the game. Your key and joystick bindings aren't touched.
Game: set the volume of the game's ambience, your footsteps, your heartbeat and the music, then the health format and shield format, which choose whether H, M, the session statuses and a health potion speak your health and shield as a percentage, which is how they start, an amount, or both. Next, choose whether things behind you sound a little lower in pitch, which is on by default and helps you tell what's behind you from what's ahead, and whether escape asks before quitting a run. If you switch off the escape question, escape leaves the run straight away. Last, choose whether your session results come up after you die, whether the menu that follows offers to copy or export them, and whether a practice death offers its retry options, all toggled on by default.
Joystick: choose whether your joystick vibrates when you're hit, which is on by default, then rebind your joystick's buttons, or view every joystick binding, by button name. The rebind list works like the keyboard's below: select an action, then add another button to it, change its button, or remove its buttons, and each change is saved as soon as you make it. When asked, press any button on your joystick; pressing escape, or nothing for 10 seconds, cancels. If the button already belongs to another action, you're asked whether to replace it, then whether to change its trigger from press to down or release.
Keyboard: rebind your keys, or view every key binding. In the rebind list, select an action, then add another key to it, change its key, or remove its keys, and each change is saved as soon as you make it. A key can be a combination: hold any shift, control or alt keys first, then press the other key last. Holding one side, such as the left shift, binds that side only, while holding both shifts, both controls or both alts binds either side. Some keyboards can't send both sides of a modifier together with every key, so if nothing happens, try one side or a different key. If the key already belongs to another action, you're asked whether to replace it, which takes it off only that action, then whether to change its trigger from press to repeat, down or release. An action left with no keys stays in the list, so you can give it one later.
Menu: turn on wrapping, first letter navigation, position information and side scrolling in menus, and choose whether the learn sounds menu tells you when a sound has several versions, which is on by default.
Sound: set the menu music volume and the master game volume, which turns every sound in the game up or down together. Turn HRTF on or off, which is on by default and makes sounds in front of you, behind you and to your sides easier to place through headphones. You can also choose between fading out by volume, which is on by default, or by pitch when you leave a run, choose whether the sounds in the learn sounds menu keep looping until you move to another, which is also on by default, and pick which sound device the game plays through. Your device choice is saved straight away.
Speech: set up your voice, either your screen reader or another voice with its own rate, pitch and volume, and preview how it sounds.
During a run, F2 opens the settings with only the game, menu and speech tabs, and F1 shows your key bindings, then your joystick bindings while a joystick is connected. The run is paused while either is open, and carries on when you close it.
Your settings, including your music, ambience and menu music volumes, are remembered from one launch to the next.

Game conclusion.

Stride Rush started as a simple audio runner, a belt and a footstep on every step, and has grown into a game of environments, dozens of obstacles and items, five difficulty levels, timed runs, practice runs and a free run sandbox, with joystick support and sounds you can make your own.
Every obstacle, item, mode and feature has its own document under game in the documentation menu, so whenever something new turns up in your runs, you can find out how it works there.

Whether you're jumping your first hazards or chasing a faster run on chaos, we hope this document gives you everything you need to keep up your stride.

Thanks for playing, and happy sprinting!
