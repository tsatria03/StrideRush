Difficulty.

Endless runs and timed runs come in five difficulty levels: easy, medium, hard, insane and chaos. Each level sets how fast you start and how fast you can go, how much each obstacle hurts, how much room you get between obstacles, how often help turns up, and when each kind of obstacle joins the run.

Starting and top speeds.

Each level starts you at a different speed: easy at 2 steps a second, medium at 2.2, hard at 2.5, insane at 2.9 and chaos at 3.3. From there, every level speeds up the same way, a little every 10 seconds, up to that level's top speed: 5 steps a second on easy, 6.7 on medium, 10 on hard, 13.3 on insane and 20 on chaos. Once you reach it, the speed holds and no more real doors come.

Damage and toughness.

The easier the level, the less each obstacle hurts and the easier fake walls, fake doors, vehicles and aircrafts are to break: easy takes 60 percent of the full damage and toughness, medium 70, hard 80, insane 90 and chaos the full amount. The damage given in each obstacle's document is the full amount. Practice runs always use the full amount.
Harder levels also have taller ladders, from 6 to 8 rungs on easy up to 10 to 12 on chaos, with a quicker pace between rungs, and bigger floor breakers, from 3 or 4 pieces on easy up to 7 or 8 on chaos.

Room between obstacles.

The easier the level, the more room you get between obstacles. On easy, there are 2 to 5 seconds of plain running between one obstacle and the next one's countdown, a quarter of a second less at each end on every level after it, down to 1 to 4 seconds on chaos. That spacing stays the same for the whole run.
Combinations come more often on harder levels, from 1 obstacle in 20 on easy to 1 in 4 on chaos.

Help along the way.

Harder levels give you fewer life capsules, steel shields and health potions: easy has 25 percent of the most there could be, medium 20, hard 15, insane 10 and chaos just 5, so taking every hit wears you down sooner or later on every level. Slides and trampolines come less often on each harder level too. Ammo packs, keys, player teleporters and powerups come just as often on every level.

When things join.

On easy to insane, not everything comes at you from the start. Hazards, spikes and mines are there from the beginning, then a new kind of obstacle joins as you speed up:

Projectiles at 2.2 steps a second.
Force fields at 2.5.
Animals at 2.9.
Fake walls, slides and trampolines at 3.3.
Fake doors, real walls, floor breakers and insects at 4.
Barriers, winds, vehicles and aircrafts at 5.

Health potions, life capsules and steel shields are there from the start, and a key always comes before each real door, while ammo packs join at 3.3 steps a second, player teleporters at 4 and powerups at 5. A level that starts faster already has everything it has passed, so insane opens with six kinds of obstacle. Chaos ignores all this and throws everything at you from the first step.

Unlocking levels.

Easy is always open, and each level after it opens once you've finished 5 endless runs on the level below. A run only counts if you opened a real door and got through it before you died, so quitting, restarting or dying before your first door doesn't count, and practice runs never do. The difficulty menu tells you how each level stands, such as "Easy. Available." or "Medium. Unlocked after completing 3 more easy runs.", and choosing a locked level tells you the same and leaves you in the menu. Your progress is remembered from one launch to the next.
Timed runs use the same five levels with their own unlocks, as described under timed runs.
