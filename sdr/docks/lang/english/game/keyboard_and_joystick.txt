Keyboard and joystick.

You can play Stride Rush with the keyboard, or with a joystick or game controller, in every menu and in a run. This document lists every key and button, including the ones you can pick up later, such as the readouts and the volume keys.

Keyboard commands.

These are the keys to start with, and you can change any of them in the keyboard tab of the settings menu, though not during a run. Most keys repeat while you hold them, at your keyboard's repeat rate, and you can change any key's trigger in the keyboard tab of the settings menu. Because left and right shift also change lanes, any command that uses shift during a run, such as shift plus tab, moves you a lane as well. These are the following:

Space, up arrow or letter W: Jumps.
Alt, down arrow or letter S: Crouches, to duck under projectiles and to fight insects.
Left shift, left arrow or letter Q: Moves one lane to the left.
Right shift, right arrow or letter E: Moves one lane to the right.
Letter A: Uses your left hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Letter D: Uses your right hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Both hand keys together, A and D: Claps, to scare away an animal. With nothing for your hands to do, a single hand makes a whoosh and a clap is just a clap.
Shift plus tab or letter I: Moves to the previous item in your inventory.
Tab or letter O: Moves to the next item in your inventory.
Enter or letter U: Uses the item you're on in your inventory, such as opening an ammo pack, unlocking a door with a key, switching a shield on or off, using a life capsule, a health potion or a teleporter.
Numbers 1 through 0: Draws the bat, boot, club, dagger, fist, hammer, knife, sword, machine gun or pistol.
Control or letter F: Swings or fires your weapon. With the machine gun, it keeps firing.
Letter R: Reloads your gun.
Letter X: Announces how much ammo your gun has loaded and in reserve.
Letter H: Announces how much health you have left, and how many lives you have, as a percentage, an amount such as 350 of 500, or both, whichever you choose in the game tab of the settings menu.
Letter M: Announces how strong your shield is, and whether it's off, as a percentage, an amount such as 320 of 400 endurance, or both, whichever you choose in the game tab of the settings menu.
Letter L: Announces which lane you're in.
Letter Z: Announces how far you've run, in steps.
Letter C: Announces your current speed, in steps a second.
Letter T: Announces how long you've been running for, or in a timed run, how much time is left on the clock and how long until you reach the finish line.
Letter B: Announces your heart rate, in beats per minute.
Letter V: Announces which environment you're in.
Letter Y: Announces how many gold coins you have available to spend.
Page up or F4: Raises the in-game music volume by 1 decibel.
Page down or F3: Lowers the in-game music volume by 1 decibel.
Home or F6: Raises the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel.
End or F5: Lowers the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel.
Letter P: Brings up the pause menu. The pause menu lets you resume, restart or quit the run, view your session statuses, and in an endless run, view, copy or export your session results. Pressing escape in the pause menu also resumes the run.
Escape: Asks whether you want to quit the run, or quits it straight away if you've turned the question off in the settings menu.
F1: Shows every key binding, and every joystick binding while a joystick is connected, with the run paused until you close it.
F2: Opens the settings menu with its game, menu and speech tabs, with the run paused until you close it.
Backslash or F7: In a free run, opens the free run console, with the run paused until you close it.
Slash or F8: In an endless or free run, opens the player shop, with the run paused until you leave it.

Speech buffer commands.

Everything the game speaks during a run is kept in speech buffers, so you can go back and review it. The all buffer holds every message, and the rest each hold one kind: achievements, for the achievements you earn; doors, for locked, jammed and unlocked doors; items, for what you pick up, your inventory and what your items do; powerups, for the powerups you collect and when they wear off; practice, for the hints in a practice run; status, for your health, shield, lane, distance, speed and running time; weapons, for drawing, reloading and your ammo; and misc, for your music and ambience volume, the environments you enter, and anything you unlock in a practice run. A buffer only shows up once something has been spoken into it, and they come in alphabetical order after all. These are the following:

Comma: Moves to the previous message in the current buffer.
Period: Moves to the next message in the current buffer.
Shift plus comma: Moves to the first message in the current buffer.
Shift plus period: Moves to the last message in the current buffer.
Left bracket: Moves to the previous buffer.
Right bracket: Moves to the next buffer.
Shift plus left bracket: Moves to the first buffer.
Shift plus right bracket: Moves to the last buffer.
Shift plus letter M: Mutes or unmutes the current buffer. A muted buffer stays muted the next time you play, until you unmute it.
Shift plus letter C: Copies the current message to the clipboard.
Alt plus shift plus letter C: Opens the current message in a box you can read with the arrow keys.
Shift plus letter B: Brings up the buffer menu, where you can copy, export, mute or review a buffer or a message.
Shift plus backslash: Exports the current buffer to a text file, then clears it.

Joystick commands.

Stride Rush works with most game controllers, such as Xbox, PlayStation and Switch Pro controllers. Plug one in before or after starting the game: plugging one in or unplugging it is announced once, and unplugging it during a run pauses the run. In a run, these are the buttons to start with, and you can change them in the joystick tab of the settings menu. The left stick always works the same as the d-pad. If your controller can vibrate, it gives a light tap when your shield takes a blow, a rumble when you're hurt, a stronger one when you lose a life, and the strongest when you die, so the harder it shakes, the worse the blow. These are the following:

D-pad up: Jumps.
D-pad down: Crouches, to duck under projectiles and to fight insects.
D-pad left: Moves one lane to the left.
D-pad right: Moves one lane to the right.
Left shoulder: Uses your left hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Right shoulder: Uses your right hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Both shoulders together: Claps, to scare away an animal.
Left stick click: Moves to the previous item in your inventory.
Right stick click: Moves to the next item in your inventory.
B button: Uses the item you're on in your inventory.
A button: Swings or fires your weapon. With the machine gun, it keeps firing.
X button: Reloads your gun.
Y button: Announces how much ammo your gun has loaded and in reserve.
Start: Brings up the pause menu.
Back: Asks whether you want to quit the run, or quits it straight away if you've turned the question off in the settings menu.

Choosing a weapon, the readouts, the volume, F1, F2, the free run console and the speech buffers have no button to start with, but you can give any of them one.

Menu buttons.

In every menu and dialog, the buttons are always these, and can't be changed. Text boxes still need the keyboard. These are the following:

D-pad or left stick: Moves, like the arrow keys. Hold it to keep moving.
A button: Chooses, like enter.
B button: Goes back, like escape.
Right shoulder: Moves to the next control, like tab.
Left shoulder: Moves to the previous control, like shift plus tab.
Y button: In a message that says to press enter to continue, copies the message, like the letter C.

The joystick.ini file.

Your joystick's buttons are kept in joystick.ini, in the data/config/main folder, which you can also edit by hand. Each line is an action, an equals sign, then its buttons by name, separated by commas, such as jump=DPAD_UP, the same way keyboard.ini names its keys. The names are A, B, X and Y for the four face buttons, BACK, GUIDE and START, LEFT_STICK and RIGHT_STICK for the stick clicks, LEFT_SHOULDER and RIGHT_SHOULDER, and DPAD_UP, DPAD_DOWN, DPAD_LEFT and DPAD_RIGHT, in capitals or not; any other button your controller has, such as a touchpad or a mute button, is BUTTON_ followed by its number, such as BUTTON_15.
A button can end with :down, :release or :up, such as reload=X:release, and with none, it's a press, though fire and duck ignore this, since each handles its own triggers. A file with the old numbers instead of names still works. If joystick.ini is missing or can't be read, the game tells you when it starts, then runs with the keyboard only.
