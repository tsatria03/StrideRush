Customizing sounds.

Every sound in Stride Rush is a file in the sounds folder, and you can change any of them. There are no sound packs to choose between: what's in that folder is what you hear.

The sounds folder.

The sounds folder has two parts: sounds/game holds the environments, weapons and items, and sounds/main holds the game's own events, your own sounds and the menus.

Changing and adding sounds.

Any sound can be replaced, and any sound can be given more versions. The game looks for a sound by its name, such as fire or step, and plays a random file whose name contains it, so adding fire8.ogg next to fire1 to fire7 adds another version of that sound, with nothing else to change.

Folders the game finds on its own.

Some folders are found on their own, so a new folder there is all it takes. Every folder in sounds/game/environments is an environment. Inside one, each folder in platforms is a surface you can run on, platforms/ledge holds the ledge around hazards, and audio/tracks and audio/ambiences hold its music and ambiences.
Its traps and transitions folders hold a folder for each kind of obstacle, such as traps/hazards or transitions/doors, each with a countdown folder for its warning sounds and a general folder of themes, where every new folder is another version of that obstacle. Creatures, such as animals, live one level down, in traps/npc/animals, and work the same way. Its vehicles folder does too, with a folder in general for each kind of vehicle.
New key colours can be added as folders in sounds/game/equipments/items/other.

Fixed folders.

Other folders are fixed: you can swap the files in them, but new folders are ignored. These are:

The kinds of obstacle themselves, since the game only knows its own, and a traps/lasers or traps/npc/robots folder would do nothing.
The ten weapons in sounds/game/equipments/weapons.
The five items in sounds/game/equipments/items/common.
sounds/main/misc/game, for powerups and the game's own events.
sounds/main/misc/player, for your own sounds.
The sounds/main/ui folders, for menus, dialogs and the speech buffers.

Making an environment.

An environment doesn't need everything. Nothing is ever borrowed from another environment: a kind of obstacle with no folder there just doesn't come while you're there, and a missing sound is simply silent.
Endless and timed runs only go to an environment once it has an obstacle they can bring, while a free run can go to any of them, so you can try out a new environment's surfaces, music and ambience before giving it any obstacles. Learn sounds finds what you add too.
