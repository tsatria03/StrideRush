Movement.

Once a run starts, you never stop moving. The belt carries you straight ahead with a footstep on every step, and you get past what's coming by jumping, crouching and switching between the belt's three lanes.

Running along the belt.

You'll hear a footstep with every step you take. The ground under your feet changes every 10 to 20 seconds, so listen for new footstep sounds as you go.
The belt speeds up as you run, a little every 10 seconds, up to your level's top speed, as described under difficulty. Press C to hear your current speed in steps a second, and Z to hear how far you've run, in steps.

Jumping.

Press the up arrow or W to jump, or D-pad up on a joystick. A jump lasts about a second, and you can't jump again until you land. When you land, you'll hear the ground you came down on.
Jumping gets you over hazards, spikes and mines. If you're still on the ground when you step onto one of those, it will catch you.

Crouching.

Hold the down arrow or S to crouch, or D-pad down on a joystick. Your footsteps go quiet while you're crouched, and you stand back up when you let go, or on your own after a second and a half. After standing up, you have to wait half a second before you can crouch again. Pressing jump while crouched jumps straight away.
Crouching gets you under projectiles, lets your weapons reach insects, and braces you against a wind.

Lanes.

The belt has three lanes, left, middle and right, and every run starts in the middle one. Press the left arrow or Q to move one lane to the left, and the right arrow or E to move one lane to the right, or D-pad left and right on a joystick. You'll hear yourself turn from the lane you move into, and if you try to go past the left or right edge of the belt, you'll bump into it and stay where you are. Press L to hear which lane you're in.
You can switch at any time, even in the middle of a jump or a crouch, except while a door carries you or you're rebuilding a floor breaker. Switching lanes while you climb a ladder makes you fall off.
Most obstacles fill the whole belt, so they always sound straight ahead of you whichever lane you're in, and switching lanes won't get you past them. Barriers, winds, vehicles, lane trampolines and items are different: each one sits in a single lane. One in your lane sounds straight ahead, one a lane over sounds off to that side, and one two lanes over sounds further out still. Powerups fill the whole belt, so you can collect one from any lane.
When two things that sound the same come side by side, one on each side of you, your ears can blend them into a single sound that seems to come from straight ahead. Switch lanes for a moment, and two side by side split apart straight away.

Movement in a run.

Each step counts toward steps ran in your statistics, and each lane switch toward lanes changed, while time ran and top speed keep track of how long and how fast you went.
Some powerups change how you move for a while: Moon Jump and Short Hop make your jumps longer or shorter, Long Crouch and Stiff Knees make your crouches longer or shorter, and Slow Motion and Rush Hour make the belt slower or faster.

Movement in practice and free runs.

A practice run keeps the belt at the speed you choose, or rising from 2 steps a second.

In a free run, you start standing still, and every key still works, so you can try jumping, crouching or your hands in place. Choose start running in the console when you're ready, and modify running speed sets how fast the belt moves.
