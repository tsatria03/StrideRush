Welcome to Stride Rush!
In this audio game, you ride a belt that carries you forward one step at a time, and you have to jump over, duck under, dodge, switch off, open or smash through everything that gets in your way.

Getting started.

From the main menu, choose game menu, then play game, then practice run, and pick hazards, the only obstacle open at first, where nothing can end your run. Once you've jumped over 5 hazards, endless run opens, so go back to play game, choose endless run, and pick easy, the only difficulty open at first. Learn sounds, in the game menu, lets you hear every obstacle's countdown before you meet it in a run.
The belt carries you forward on its own, so you never press a key to move ahead. Listen in front of you: every obstacle plays a five-cue countdown as it comes, and on easy you have time to act on the last few cues.
Press the up arrow or W to jump over hazards, spikes and mines, and hold the down arrow or S to crouch under projectiles.
Press the left and right arrows, or Q and E, to switch lanes out of a barrier's way.
Press A for your left hand and D for your right, to switch off force fields, pick up items and open doors, and press both together to clap animals away.
Press space to swing or fire your weapon at fake walls and fake doors, and the number keys to choose a different weapon.
Press shift plus tab and tab, or I and O, to move through your inventory, and enter or U to use an item. Before each real door, pick up its key, then use it to unlock the door.
Press P to pause, or escape to quit the run.

The sections below explain every obstacle, item and feature in detail, and the keyboard commands just below list every key, including the ones you can pick up later, such as the readouts and the volume keys.

Keyboard commands

Up arrow or letter W: Jumps.
Down arrow or letter S: Crouches, to duck under projectiles.
Left arrow or letter Q: Moves one lane to the left.
Right arrow or letter E: Moves one lane to the right.
Letter A: Uses your left hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Letter D: Uses your right hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item.
Both hand keys together, A and D: Claps, to scare away an animal. With nothing for your hands to do, a single hand makes a whoosh and a clap is just a clap.
Shift plus tab or letter I: Moves to the previous item in your inventory.
Tab or letter O: Moves to the next item in your inventory.
Enter or letter U: Uses the item you're on in your inventory, such as opening an ammo pack, unlocking a door with a key, putting on a shield, using a life capsule, a health potion or a teleporter.
Numbers 1 through 0: Draws the bat, boot, club, feat, fist, hammer, knife, sword, machine gun or pistol.
Space: Swings or fires your weapon. With the machine gun, it keeps firing.
Letter R: Reloads your gun.
Letter X: Announces how much ammo your gun has loaded and in reserve.
Letter H: Announces how much health you have left, as a percentage, and how many lives you have.
Letter M: Announces how strong the shield you're wearing is, as a percentage.
Letter L: Announces which lane you're in.
Letter Z: Announces how far you've run, in steps.
Letter C: Announces your current speed, in steps a second.
Letter T: Announces how long you've been running for.
Letter B: Announces your heart rate, in beats per minute.
Letter V: Announces which environment you're in.
Page up or F4: Raises the in-game music volume by 1 decibel.
Page down or F3: Lowers the in-game music volume by 1 decibel.
Home or F6: Raises the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel.
End or F5: Lowers the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel.
Letter P: Brings up the pause menu. The pause menu lets you resume, restart or quit the run, and in an endless run, view, copy or export your session results. Pressing escape in the pause menu also resumes the run.
Escape: Asks whether you want to quit the run, or quits it straight away if you've turned the question off in the settings menu.
F1: Shows every key binding, with the run paused until you close it.
F2: Opens the settings menu with its game, menu and speech tabs, with the run paused until you close it.

Speech buffer commands

Everything the game speaks during a run is kept in speech buffers, so you can go back and review it. The all buffer holds every message, and the rest each hold one kind: achievements, for the achievements you earn; doors, for locked, jammed and unlocked doors; items, for what you pick up, your inventory and what your items do; powerups, for the powerups you collect and when they wear off; practice, for the hints in a practice run; status, for your health, shield, lane, distance, speed and running time; weapons, for drawing, reloading and your ammo; and misc, for your music and ambience volume, the environments you enter, and anything you unlock in a practice run. A buffer only shows up once something has been spoken into it, and they come in alphabetical order after all.

Comma: Moves to the previous message in the current buffer.
Period: Moves to the next message in the current buffer.
Shift plus comma: Moves to the first message in the current buffer.
Shift plus period: Moves to the last message in the current buffer.
Left bracket: Moves to the previous buffer.
Right bracket: Moves to the next buffer.
Shift plus left bracket: Moves to the first buffer.
Shift plus right bracket: Moves to the last buffer.
Shift plus letter M: Mutes or unmutes the current buffer. A muted buffer stays muted the next time you play, until you unmute it.
Shift plus letter C: Copies the current message to the clipboard.
Alt plus shift plus letter C: Opens the current message in a box you can read with the arrow keys.
Shift plus letter B: Brings up the buffer menu, where you can copy, export, mute or review a buffer or a message.
Shift plus backslash: Exports the current buffer to a text file, then clears it.

How to play.

Once a run starts, you never stop moving. The belt carries you straight ahead, and you'll hear a footstep with every step you take. The ground under your feet changes every 10 to 20 seconds, so listen for new footstep sounds as you go.
You start at a speed of 2 steps a second on easy, or faster on the harder difficulty levels, described below. Every 10 seconds you get a little faster, until you reach your level's top speed, described below.
Obstacles are heard in front of you, about 3 seconds before you reach them, getting louder as they come closer, and once you're past one you'll hear it fall behind you, a little lower in pitch. Jump at the right moment to sail over most of them. If you're still on the ground when you step onto one, it will catch you. There are seven exceptions: you crouch under projectiles, you switch off force fields with one of your hands, you scare animals away by clapping, you break fake walls with your weapons, you climb real walls by their ladders, you open doors with your hands, and you dodge barriers and winds by switching lanes.
A jump lasts about a second. You can't jump again until you land, and when you land you'll hear the ground you came down on.
Runs are endless. The goal is to get as far as you can before something stops you.
Sometimes two obstacles come as a combination, close together, so you need two actions in a row, such as crouching under a projectile and then jumping a spike, or switching lanes out of a barrier's way and then breaking a fake wall. Each one plays its own countdown, the two overlapping, and the one whose countdown finishes first comes first. The gap between them always leaves you time, so after a jump you land before the second one arrives, and a combination's barrier is always in your lane. Combinations only start once both of their obstacles have joined the run, never include real doors, real walls, winds, slides or trampolines, and come more often on harder levels, from 1 obstacle in 20 on easy to 1 in 4 on chaos. Getting through both without a hit counts as a combination cleared.
Slides cover the whole belt, so you always go onto one. You'll hear it coming ahead of you, then a sound as you get on, and for 2 to 4 seconds it carries you along at twice your speed. While you're on it you can't jump, crouch, use your hands, your inventory or your weapons, but you can switch lanes. Obstacles still come, without their countdowns, and you pass straight through them without being hit, while any item that reaches you in the lane you're in is caught for you, so steer into the lanes of the items you hear. Your footsteps and heartbeat go quiet until you hear it end, and nothing waits right after a slide, so the next obstacle always gives you its full warning. Slides only start once the belt reaches 3.3 steps a second, and they come less often on each harder difficulty.
Trampolines come in four kinds. A lane trampoline sits in one lane and is heard in that lane as you come toward it, so you take it by being in its lane and avoid it by being in another one. A belt-wide trampoline covers the whole belt and is heard straight ahead, so there's no avoiding it. Jumping never gets you over a trampoline, since running onto one launches you whether you're on the ground or in the air. A good trampoline sounds normal and sends you on a long flight of two and a half seconds. Everything you reach before you land passes under you without a sound, while anything past your landing counts down as normal, and in the air you can switch lanes, use your hands, fire and use your inventory, though you can't jump or crouch until you land. A worn trampoline sounds higher or lower, like a fake door, and gives you only a normal jump. Trampolines start once the belt reaches 3.3 steps a second, and like slides they come less often on each harder difficulty.
Every run takes place in an environment, a place with its own ground, music, ambience and obstacle sounds. A run starts in a random one, and every 1 to 2 minutes it moves you to a different one, telling you which, such as "You've entered the uncharted environment." The ground and the ambience change straight away, and the obstacles that come next belong to the new place, while anything already on its way keeps its own sounds. The song that's playing finishes first, and the next one comes from the new environment's music. If a run has only one environment to be in, its ambience fades into another of that place's ambiences every 1 to 2 minutes instead.
Every run has music and an ambience playing behind it. The music shuffles through its tracks, playing one after another, and every run starts a new shuffle. The ambience puts you somewhere, such as a city street, a beach or a forest in the rain. Change the music volume with page up and page down or F4 and F3, and the ambience volume with home and end or F6 and F5. Each change is spoken, and a key does nothing once its volume is as high or as low as it goes.
You can also hear your heartbeat, which speeds up as you run faster. It starts at 5 beats per minute and climbs steadily as the belt speeds up, reaching 155 beats per minute at 6.7 steps a second, where it stays however much faster you go. A speed powerup changes it too, for as long as the powerup lasts. Press B to hear your heart rate, and change the heartbeat volume with its slider in the game tab of the settings menu. The heartbeat goes quiet while a door carries you, like your footsteps, and pauses with everything else.

Lanes.

The belt has three lanes, left, middle and right, and every run starts in the middle one. Press the left arrow or Q to move one lane to the left, and the right arrow or E to move one lane to the right. You can switch at any time, even in the middle of a jump or a crouch, except while a door is carrying you. You'll hear yourself turn from the lane you move into, and if you try to go past the left or right edge of the belt, you'll bump into it and stay where you are. Press L to hear which lane you're in.
Most obstacles fill the whole belt, so they always sound straight ahead of you whichever lane you're in, and switching lanes won't get you past them. Barriers and items are different: each one sits in a single lane. One in your lane sounds straight ahead, one a lane over sounds off to that side, and one two lanes over sounds further out still. Powerups fill the whole belt like obstacles, so you can collect one from any lane.
Sometimes two barriers or two items turn up side by side in different lanes. When two sound the same, one on each side of you, your ears can blend them into a single sound that seems to come from straight ahead, even though nothing is in your lane. There are two ways to tell. A barrier in your lane always plays its countdown, so a barrier that sounds centred but has no countdown means there's one on each side of you, and the lane you're in is the open one. For items, or whenever you're not sure, switch lanes for a moment: two side by side split apart straight away, one coming in front of you and the other going far off to the side.

Obstacles.

Hazards, such as pits, chasms, fire, lava, water, quicksand and electricity, end your run straight away if you fall into one. Each hazard has a five-step ledge leading up to it, and as you step along the ledge you'll hear a five-cue countdown, one cue per step, with the last cue on the step right before the hazard. At slower speeds, wait for the last few cues before you jump. Once you're fast enough, jumping on any ledge step will carry you over.
Spikes swing back and forth with their own rhythm, which you'll hear as you approach them. Getting hit by spikes takes 20 of your health.
Mines make a sound when they appear, then light up and start ticking once you get close. Stepping onto a mine takes 60 of your health. Once you jump over a mine, you'll hear it keep ticking as it fades out behind you.
Force fields hum ahead of you while they're on, and jumping or crouching won't get you past one. Press either hand key during a force field's countdown to switch it off. You'll hear the switch, then the field's sound changes as it shuts off, and you can run straight through. At the fastest speeds, where the countdown is very short, you can still switch one off in the last half second before you reach it. If you reach a force field while it's still on, it takes 30 of your health and throws you 5 steps forward past it.
Projectiles, such as missiles, fly toward you at head height, twice as fast as you run, so you'll hear them rushing in. Hold the crouch key so you're crouched when one reaches you, and it flies over you and fades away behind. A crouch only lasts a second and a half, so at slower speeds wait for the last few countdown cues before you crouch. Jumping won't help, and if one hits you, it takes 40 of your health.
Fake walls are glass windows standing in your way, and you'll hear them ahead of you as you get closer, each one a little higher or lower in pitch than normal, like a fake door. Jumping or crouching won't get you past one, so swing or fire your weapon at it as it comes. Your weapons reach about 2 seconds ahead of you, so start early. Each hit cracks it a little more, and once it shatters you can run straight through. Fake walls get tougher the faster you run: early walls break with a few swings of the bat, while the fastest ones take a few hits from a strong weapon. If you crash into a fake wall that's still standing, it takes 50 of your health.
Real walls are tall walls you can't break, played at their normal pitch, so you can tell them from fake walls. Each one has a ladder 5 steps in front of it, which counts down like any obstacle. Press either hand during its countdown to grab it, and once you reach it the belt holds you while you climb. Climb hand over hand, pressing the other hand from your last each time, at a steady pace: each rung must come within 2 seconds of the last on easy, down to 1 second on chaos, but no sooner than 0.2 seconds on easy, up to 0.4 on chaos. Harder levels have taller ladders, from 6 to 8 rungs on easy up to 10 to 12 on chaos, so listen for the top instead of counting. Reach it and you climb over, landing past the wall. While you climb, you can't jump, crouch, use your weapons or your inventory.
If you run into the ladder without grabbing it, press the same hand twice, or take too long on a rung, the ladder collapses on you, taking 60 of your health. If you switch lanes on the ladder, or climb too fast, you fall off, taking 40. Either way, you then run on into the real wall, which takes 50 more. On a slide or a trampoline flight, a ladder and its wall pass you by harmlessly.
Animals, birds and dogs, call out ahead of you, and every one has its own call. Jumping, crouching or a single hand won't get rid of one: clap by pressing both hand keys together during an animal's countdown, and it runs off, so you can run straight on. If an animal reaches you, it runs into you and takes 10 of your health.
Barriers, such as cars, boulders and firewalls, each fill a single lane, and you'll hear them in their own lane as they come. Jumping, crouching, your hands and your weapons are no use against one: the only way past is to be in another lane when it reaches you. Each one turns up in a random lane, and sometimes two come side by side, but never in all three lanes, so there's always a lane open. If a barrier reaches you in its lane, it takes 80 of your health and carries on past you, fading away behind. Since many of them pass in another lane, barriers come about twice as often as each other kind of obstacle.
Winds are gusts that blow toward you down a single lane, a little faster than the belt carries you, and chase you. Each one starts in a random lane, and about every three quarters of a second it moves one lane toward the lane you're in, so you'll hear it sweep across the belt until it's lined up with you. Its countdown only plays while it's in your lane. Switching lanes early doesn't help, since it just follows you, but switching at the last moment, after it's made its final move, leaves it behind to blow past you. If it reaches you in your lane, it takes 5 of your health and shoves you one lane over, toward the middle from either side lane, or the way it was blowing from the middle lane, which could put you in the path of something else. Crouching as it reaches you braces you, so you stay in your lane, though you still take the hit.
Doors mark your progress. As the belt picks up the pace, a real door comes up ahead of you every so often, marking the start of a faster stretch. Real doors are locked, so you'll need the key that turns up earlier in each stretch. Once the door is within about 2 seconds of you, choose a key in your inventory and press U to unlock it, and you'll hear the lock give. Then press either hand key in the last few steps before the door to open it, and the door carries you through into the next stretch. If you try to open a door that's still locked, you'll hear it refuse. While it moves you, your footsteps go quiet and you can't jump, crouch, switch lanes, use your weapons or use your inventory, until it closes behind you. Once you reach the top speed, there are no more real doors.
Watch out for fake doors, though, which can turn up anywhere in a stretch. They sound slightly higher or lower than a real door, and they won't open: your hands or a key just find them jammed, and keys aren't used up on them. Break them with your weapons the same way as walls, but they're much tougher, about twice as tough as a wall at any speed, so draw your strongest weapon and start early. Doors, real and fake, have a countdown too, so you'll hear them coming. Crashing into any door, real or fake, takes 70 of your health.
Spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles, fake walls, ladders, animals, barriers and doors have the same five-cue countdown as hazards, at every speed, but without a ledge. A fake wall's and a ladder's countdown play two notes at once, an animal's and a barrier's three, and a door's four. A barrier's countdown only plays while you're in its lane, so it stops as soon as you move out of the way and starts again if you move back in. Once a fake wall is broken, a ladder grabbed, a force field switched off, an animal scared away, a real door opened or a fake one broken, its countdown stops. Each kind of obstacle has its own countdown sounds, so you can tell which one is coming from the countdown alone.

Health and lives.

You start each endless run with 1 life and 200 health. The damage each obstacle does, given above, is the full amount, and easier levels take less, as described under difficulty. Spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles, fake walls, ladders, real walls, animals, barriers and doors each take some of your health when they catch you, and you'll hear yourself get hit. When your health runs out, you lose a life: if you have lives to spare, you'll hear a sound, your health fills back up to 200, and you keep running. If it was your last life, the run ends, and you can see your results and replay it, as described under menus. Hazards are different: falling into one ends your run straight away, however much health or how many lives you have. A steel shield takes those hits before your health does, health potions heal you a little, and life capsules give you more lives. Press H to hear how much health you have left, and how many lives.

Crouching.

You can crouch by holding down the crouch key. Your footsteps go quiet while you're crouched, and you stand back up when you let go, or on your own after a second and a half. After standing up, you have to wait half a second before you can crouch again. Pressing the jump key while crouched jumps straight away. Crouching is how you get under projectiles.

Weapons.

You have ten weapons, one on each number key from 1 to 0: the bat, boot, club, feat, fist, hammer, knife, sword, machine gun and pistol. Each run starts with the fist in your hands, and each weapon is stronger than the one before it. Press the space bar to swing a melee weapon or fire a gun. A melee weapon can swing up to five times a second and the pistol fire up to ten times a second, one swing or shot per press, while the machine gun keeps firing ten times a second for as long as you hold the space bar. A melee weapon hits straight away, while a gun fires a bullet that you'll hear flying off ahead of you until it lands. A bullet only flies as far as your weapons reach, so a shot fired at something still too far away falls short and wastes the round.
The machine gun and pistol use ammo. Each starts every run with 50 rounds loaded and 50 in reserve, and they keep their own ammo separately. A reserve can hold up to 200 rounds. Once a gun is empty you'll hear it click instead of firing. You can reload any time a gun isn't full, and you'll hear a ping when it's ready. When the reserve runs out, that gun is empty until you open an ammo pack.
Your weapons are for breaking fake walls and fake doors, and they all reach the same distance ahead of you. Real doors and real walls can't be harmed. The stronger the weapon, the fewer hits a fake wall takes. Your weapons always hit the nearest thing ahead of you, and that includes any item lying ahead of you in your lane, such as an ammo pack, key or shield, which breaks if you hit it. Items in other lanes are safe from your weapons.

Items and your inventory.

Every so often an ammo pack turns up ahead of you instead of an obstacle, in one of the three lanes, and you'll hear it waiting there. Get into its lane, then press either hand key in the last few steps before you reach it to pick it up, and it goes into your inventory. Be careful: if you run onto a pack in its lane, or land on it from a jump, it breaks and is lost, and so does shooting or swinging at it. A jump that carries you right over it just misses it, and a pack in another lane just passes you by. Sometimes two items turn up side by side in different lanes, so you have to choose which one to go for.
Packs stay in your inventory until you open them. Opening one adds 5 to 10 rounds to the reserve of the gun you're holding, so draw the gun you want to fill first. If you're holding a melee weapon, or that gun's reserve is already full, the pack stays in your inventory for later. Your inventory starts empty every run.
Keys turn up once in every stretch, somewhere before its real door, and you pick them up and lose them the same way as ammo packs. They come in several colors, but the color doesn't matter: any key unlocks any real door. Each key you use is gone, and a key you don't use stays in your inventory for a later door. If there's no door within reach, the door ahead is already unlocked, or it's a fake door, the key stays in your inventory.
Steel shields turn up only now and then, much less often than ammo packs, and less often still on harder difficulties, and you pick them up and lose them the same way. Choose one in your inventory and press U to wear it like armor. You can only wear one at a time, so any others wait in your inventory until the one you're wearing breaks. A shield takes the damage from spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles, walls, animals, barriers and doors in place of your health. You'll hear the obstacle hit your shield instead of you, and a force field still throws you forward. A fresh shield takes 400 damage, twice your own health: that's 40 animals, 20 spikes, 14 force fields, 10 projectiles, 8 walls, 7 mines, 6 door crashes or 5 barriers, and the hit that wears it out is still fully blocked, so you'll hear it break and lose nothing. Shields can't save you from hazards. Press M to hear how strong your shield is.
Life capsules turn up only rarely, and more rarely still on harder difficulties, and you pick them up and lose them the same way as ammo packs. Use one with U and you get 1 or 2 extra lives, up to a limit of 20 lives. You can carry as many capsules as you find, and if you're already at 20 lives, it tells you so and keeps the capsule for later.
Health potions turn up more often than life capsules, and you pick them up and lose them the same way. Use one with U to heal 5 to 10 percent of your health, never past full. If your health is already full, it tells you so and keeps the potion for later, so collecting a few is worth it.
Player teleporters are a gamble. Pick one up the same way, then press U, and it throws you a random 2 to 5 seconds' worth of belt either forward or back. It never drops you right on top of an obstacle, but it can drop you just in front of one, so be ready to react the moment you land. If it sends you back, anything you passed that's still waiting there, such as a spike or a hazard, comes up again, while anything you already dealt with, such as a broken fake wall or an opened door, stays that way.

Powerups.

Every so often a powerup turns up ahead of you. Unlike items, you don't pick it up with your hands, and it doesn't go into your inventory. A ground powerup sits on the belt, and you collect it by running onto it, so jumping over it skips it. A flight powerup floats above the belt, with its own sound repeating a little more slowly, and you collect it by being in the air as you reach it, so running underneath misses it. You can't tell a good powerup from a bad one until you collect it, so going for one is a gamble. Powerups don't sit in a lane, so you can collect one whichever lane you're in.
When you collect one, you hear it and its name is spoken. Its effect lasts 15 seconds, and you're told when it wears off. Powerups come in pairs, one good and one bad, and a new one replaces whichever of its pair is already running instead of adding to it, so you can have one effect of each pair going at once.
The pairs are: Slow Motion and Rush Hour, which make the belt slower or faster; Moon Jump and Short Hop, which make your jumps longer or shorter; Long Crouch and Stiff Knees, which make your crouches longer or shorter; Quick Swings and Clumsy Swings, which make your melee weapons swing faster or slower; Rapid Fire and Sticky Trigger, which make your guns fire faster or slower; Tough Hide and Soft Hide, which make hits do half or double damage to your health; and Sturdy Shield and Brittle Shield, which make hits do half or double damage to your shield. The shield pair only turns up while you're wearing a shield, and ends early if your shield breaks.

Difficulty.

When you choose endless run, you pick one of five difficulty levels: easy, medium, hard, insane and chaos. Each one starts you at a different speed: easy at 2 steps a second, medium at 2.2, hard at 2.5, insane at 2.9 and chaos at 3.3. From there, every level speeds up the same way, a little every 10 seconds, up to that level's top speed: 5 steps a second on easy, 6.7 on medium, 10 on hard, 13.3 on insane and 20 on chaos. Once you reach it, the speed holds and no more real doors come. The easier the level, the less each obstacle hurts and the easier walls and fake doors are to break: easy takes 60 percent of the full damage and toughness, medium 70, hard 80, insane 90 and chaos the full amount. Practice runs always use the full amount.
Harder levels also give you fewer life capsules, steel shields and health potions: easy has 25 percent of the most there could be, medium 20, hard 15, insane 10 and chaos just 5, so taking every hit wears you down sooner or later on every level. Ammo packs, keys, teleporters and powerups come just as often on every level.
The easier the level, the more room you get between obstacles. On easy, there are 2 to 5 seconds of plain running between one obstacle and the next one's countdown, a quarter of a second less at each end on every level after it, down to 1 to 4 seconds on chaos. That spacing stays the same for the whole run.
On easy to insane, not everything comes at you from the start. Hazards, spikes and mines are there from the beginning, then a new kind of obstacle joins as you speed up: projectiles at 2.2 steps a second, force fields at 2.5, animals at 2.9, fake walls at 3.3, fake doors and real walls at 4, and barriers and winds at 5. Health potions, life capsules and steel shields are there from the start, and a key always comes before each real door, while ammo packs join at 3.3 steps a second, player teleporters at 4 and powerups at 5. A level that starts faster already has everything it has passed, so insane opens with six kinds of obstacle. Chaos ignores all this and throws everything at you from the first step.
Easy is always open, and each level after it opens once you've finished 5 runs on the level below. A run only counts if you opened a real door and got through it before you died, so quitting, restarting or dying before your first door doesn't count, and practice runs never do. The difficulty menu tells you how each level stands, such as "Easy. Available." or "Medium. Unlocked after completing 3 more easy runs.", and choosing a locked level tells you the same and leaves you in the menu. Your progress is remembered from one launch to the next.

Practice runs.

Practice run lets you learn one kind of obstacle at a time, for as long as you like. Choose hazards, spikes, mines, projectiles, force fields, animals, fake walls, real walls, mixed walls, fake doors, real doors, mixed doors, barriers, winds, combinations, trampolines or mixed obstacles, listed in the order they join an endless run, with combinations, trampolines and mixed obstacles last, then a speed of 2, 2.5, 3.3, 5 or 10 steps a second, or rising. The belt stays at that speed for the whole run, and only that obstacle comes at you, with the same spacing as chaos. Animals bring both birds and dogs, barriers come in random lanes and sometimes side by side, fake doors are only fake ones to break, real doors are only real ones, each with its key just before it, to unlock and open, and mixed doors mix the two, so you can practice telling them apart.
Rising starts at 2 steps a second and speeds up every 5 seconds, twice as fast as an endless run, until it reaches 10 steps a second about 3 and a half minutes in, then stays there. If you die, you try again at the speed you'd reached, while restarting the run starts you from 2 again.
Trampolines bring a trampoline of any kind every other spawn, with an obstacle of any type in between. Mixed obstacles brings every kind at random, each about as often as the others, with each real door's key just before it, as a warm-up before an endless run.
Fake walls are only fake ones to break, real walls are only real ones, each with its ladder to climb, and mixed walls mix the two, so you can practice telling them apart by their pitch.
A practice run never ends on its own. You start with 1 life and 1 steel shield in your inventory, except when practicing hazards, since a shield can't save you from one, and nothing else turns up on the belt except the keys for real doors, and no slides, and no trampolines unless you're practicing them. Hits take your health as they do in a real run, and when one would take your last life, or you fall into a hazard, you die just as you would in a real run, but the run doesn't end. You hear yourself die, and a message tells you what caught you and how to get past it. After you press enter, choose retry practice to be set down just past what caught you, with full health, your life and your shield back (if you have one), or return to game menu to stop, which escape does too. Quit from the pause menu or with escape when you're done, or restart to begin the same practice again. A practice run moves between environments too, but only ones that have the obstacle you're practicing.
You only have weapons when practicing fake walls, mixed walls, fake doors, mixed doors, combinations, trampolines or mixed obstacles, since those can need them, and then your guns never run out of reserve rounds, and X tells you so. In every other practice run, no weapon is drawn and the weapon keys do nothing.
While you practice, each thing is described in the practice buffer as it appears: what it is, which lane it's in if it sits in one, and what to do about it. Each kind is described the first 5 times it appears in a run, or just the first time in mixed obstacles, and once 5 of what you're practicing have passed you, a closing line says the hints are done. Until then, getting hit by anything but a hazard, or missing a key, starts the hints over, so you hear them again; after the closing line, the rest of the run is quiet.
At first, only hazards are open, and each obstacle after them opens once you've got past 5 of the one before it, while practicing that one: jump over 5 hazards to open spikes, 5 spikes to open mines, and 5 mines to open projectiles, duck under 5 projectiles to open force fields, switch off 5 force fields to open animals, scare off 5 animals to open fake walls, break 5 fake walls to open real walls, climb 5 real walls to open mixed walls, get through 5 mixed walls, by breaking a fake one or climbing a real one, to open fake doors, break 5 fake doors to open real doors, open 5 real doors to open mixed doors, get through 5 mixed doors, by opening a real one or breaking a fake one, to open barriers, dodge past 5 barriers to open winds, dodge 5 winds to open combinations, where every spawn is a pair of obstacles, clear 5 combinations to open trampolines, and take 5 trampoline flights to open mixed obstacles.
Jumping over your first 5 hazards also opens endless run, since a hazard is the one obstacle that ends a run outright.
Only getting past the right way counts, so a hit or a fall doesn't, but your count adds up over all your practice runs and deaths, and doesn't need to be in a row. The practice menu says each obstacle is available, or how many more of the one before it you need, and choosing a locked one tells you the same. Each time you open something, you hear a sound and are told what you've unlocked. Your unlocks are saved, and clearing your stats or progress never takes them away. If you had already played endless runs before these locks came in, endless run and every practice obstacle are open for you.

Menus.

The main menu has eight options. The documentation menu lets you read the changelog, credits, readme and todo list, each in a window you can move through with the arrow keys and close when you're done. The game menu is where you start a run, by choosing play game, learn the game's sounds, view your game achievements or statistics, or clear, copy or export all of your game progress, as described below. Play game asks which game you'd like to play: endless run, which then asks for a difficulty and opens once you've practiced hazards, or practice run, which asks for an obstacle and a speed, both described above. The settings menu, change game version and check for updates are described below too. Test speakers plays a sound so you can check your speakers or headphones, and you can press enter to end it early. Restart game closes Stride Rush and opens it again, and exit game closes it. Pressing escape on the main menu also exits.
During a run, press P for the pause menu, where you can resume, restart or quit the run. Restarting or quitting asks you first, since you'll lose the run you're on. In an endless run, the pause menu also has view session results, which lists your stats for the run so far, the same way session statistics does, and options to copy them to your clipboard or export them to a file, as described under statistics. The run stays paused while you look. Pressing escape during a run asks straight away whether you want to quit, and the run stops moving while you decide. Both the pause menu and the quit question pause every sound but the ambience, so the game is never silent while you decide. Whenever you leave a run this way, its sounds fade out.
When an endless run ends in a death, your results for the run come up straight away, starting with the difficulty and your death outcome, which tells you what killed you. Pressing escape or back then asks what you'd like to do now: copy or export your results, replay the run on the same difficulty, or return to the game menu. Copying or exporting brings you back to the same question, so you can do both before you choose. The game tab of the settings menu can turn off the results, so the question comes straight after you die, starting with "You have died", and can leave copy and export out of the question.

Learning the sounds.

Learn sounds, in the game menu, lets you play the game's sounds before you run into them. It has three categories: environments, with a category for each environment, which for now is just the default one, holding its countdowns, with the countdown of each kind of obstacle, and its traps, with a list for each kind of obstacle and animals split into birds and dogs; items, with common items and keys; and weapons, with artillery and melee. Each category tells you how many sounds it holds, and how many there are in total when some of them have several versions or layers. Everything is in alphabetical order. Escape or back takes you up one level.
In a list of sounds, press enter to play the one you're on, or hold it down to keep starting it again. A sound with several versions plays one of them at random each time. Moving to another sound stops the one playing, space pauses or resumes it, and shift plus page up or page down makes the sounds louder or quieter. A countdown plays its five cues at the starting speed, and you can keep moving through the list while it plays. Each menu remembers where you were until you close the game.

Achievements.

View game achievements, in the game menu, holds over 100 achievements to work toward. Session achievements are earned within a single run, such as opening a number of real doors before the run ends, and lifetime achievements add up over all of your runs, such as running a number of steps in total. Only endless runs count: practice runs never earn achievements. The moment you earn one, mid-run or as a run ends, a sound plays and the game tells you its name and what you did, and the message is kept in the achievements speech buffer. Each achievement is earned once and stays earned for good, unless you clear all of your game progress.
The achievements menu tells you how many you've unlocked in total, and each list tells you how many of its own you've unlocked. The lists keep every ladder in order, from the easiest step to the hardest. An earned achievement shows by name, and pressing enter on it tells you what you did to earn it. One you haven't earned yet says locked, and pressing enter on it gives you a hint about how to earn it.

Statistics.

View game statistics, in the game menu, keeps track of how you play. Only endless runs count: practice runs never change your stats. Session statistics shows your last endless run, starting with its difficulty and its death outcome, which names the obstacle that killed you, or says none if you quit or restarted, and lifetime statistics adds up every endless run you've played, starting with when you last played and how many runs you've played on each difficulty. Each stat is a short label and its value, such as "steps ran, 87", or "total steps ran, 4,000" in the lifetime list. Between them they cover your steps, running time, top speed and lane changes, every kind of obstacle you've got past and how often each one caught you, the damage you've taken and the lives you've lost, each item you've picked up, broken and used, the powerups you've collected, and your melee swings, gunshots and gun reloads. Your stats are saved whenever an endless run ends, however it ends.
Achievement statistics, first in the statistics menu, tells you how many achievements you've unlocked, then lists each one as complete, or incomplete with how far you've got, such as "side stepper, incomplete, 120 of 500 lane changes". An incomplete session achievement shows your best record, the most you've managed in a single run.
The statistics menu can also clear, copy or export your stats. Clear game statistics clears your session stats, your lifetime stats or both, after asking you first, and never clears your achievements or changes which difficulties, modes or practice obstacles you've unlocked. Copy all game statistics to clipboard copies your session and lifetime stats as one text, and export all game statistics to file saves the same text as player_statistics.txt in a stats folder beside your settings.
The game menu does the same for all of your game progress at once. Clear game progresses clears your session and lifetime stats, your best records and every achievement you've earned, after asking you first, and still never changes which difficulties, modes or practice obstacles you've unlocked. Copy all game progresses to clipboard copies your achievement stats followed by your session and lifetime stats, and export all game progresses to file saves the same text as player_progresses.txt in the same stats folder.
Your session results can be copied or exported on their own too, from the pause menu during an endless run or after a death, as described under menus. Copy session results to clipboard copies the run's stats as one text, and export session results to file saves the same text as player_results.txt in the same stats folder.

Updates.

Stride Rush can update itself. If you tick the setting in the general tab of the settings menu, it checks quietly for a new version each time it starts, and only speaks up if there is one. Choose check for updates in the main menu to check at any time, and it tells you if you already have the latest version or if it can't check right now.
When there's a new version, you're asked whether to download it, and then whether you want the Windows installer or the portable version. While it downloads, you'll hear beeps that rise as it goes. Press space for the percentage, 1, 2 and 3 for the file size, how much has downloaded and how much is left, m to mute or unmute the beeps, h to hear these keys again, or escape to cancel. Afterwards, run installer starts the installer and closes the game, so the new version replaces this one. Extract file unpacks the portable version into an sdr folder inside your game folder, then closes the game, so you can run the new version from there.
Change game version, in the main menu, lets you type a different version number for the updater to compare against, using numbers and periods only. Type an older one and check for updates offers you the latest version again. It lasts until you close the game.

Settings.

The settings menu is a window with a list of tabs at the top. Move through the tabs with the arrow keys, and tab through the options on each one. Control plus tab and control plus shift plus tab move to the next or previous tab from anywhere in the window, wrapping around at either end. Nothing you tick or slide takes effect until you choose save settings, and cancel or escape leaves everything as it was. Each volume slider goes from 0 to 100 percent in steps of 1, where 100 percent is full volume, and the volume keys and the sliders always agree, since each shows what the other last set. The menu music, music and ambience start at 80 percent, and the footsteps, heartbeat and master volumes at 100 percent.
General: choose whether the game checks for updates when it starts, which is unticked to start with. Reset settings puts every setting back to how it started, after asking you first, then restarts the game. Your key bindings aren't touched.
Game: set the volume of the game's ambience, your footsteps, your heartbeat and the music, then choose whether things behind you sound a little lower in pitch, which is ticked to start with and helps you tell what's behind you from what's ahead, and whether escape asks before quitting a run. If you untick the escape question, escape leaves the run straight away. Last, choose whether your session results come up after you die, and whether the menu that follows offers to copy or export them, both ticked to start with.
Keyboard: rebind your keys, or view every key binding. In the rebind list, select an action, then add another key to it, change its key, or remove its keys, and each change is saved as soon as you make it. A key can be a combination: hold any shift, control or alt keys first, then press the other key last. Holding one side, such as the left shift, binds that side only, while holding both shifts, both controls or both alts binds either side. Some keyboards can't send both sides of a modifier together with every key, so if nothing happens, try one side or a different key. If the key already belongs to another action, you're asked whether to replace it, which takes it off only that action, then whether to change its trigger from press to repeat, down or release. An action left with no keys stays in the list, so you can give it one later.
Menu: turn on wrapping, first letter navigation, position information and side scrolling in menus, and choose whether the learn sounds menu tells you when a sound has several versions, which is ticked to start with.
Sound: set the menu music volume and the master game volume, which turns every sound in the game up or down together. Turn HRTF on or off, which is ticked to start with and makes sounds in front of you, behind you and to your sides easier to place through headphones. You can also choose between fading out by volume, which is ticked to start with, or by pitch when you leave a run, choose whether the sounds in the learn sounds menu keep looping until you move to another, which is also ticked to start with, and pick which sound device the game plays through. Your device choice is saved straight away.
Speech: set up your voice, either your screen reader or another voice with its own rate, pitch and volume, and preview how it sounds.
During a run, F2 opens the settings with only the game, menu and speech tabs, and F1 shows your key bindings. The run is paused while either is open, and carries on when you close it.
Your settings, including your music, ambience and menu music volumes, are remembered from one launch to the next.
