Welcome to Stride Rush!
In this audio game, you ride a belt that carries you forward one step at a time, and you have to jump over, duck under, dodge, switch off, open or smash through everything that gets in your way.

How to play.

Once a run starts, you never stop moving. The belt carries you straight ahead, and you'll hear a footstep with every step you take. The ground under your feet changes every 10 to 20 seconds, so listen for new footstep sounds as you go.
You start at a speed of 500 milliseconds per step. Every 10 seconds you get a little faster, 10 milliseconds at a time, until you reach the top speed of 50 milliseconds per step.
Obstacles are heard in front of you, about 3 seconds before you reach them, getting louder as they come closer, and once you're past one you'll hear it fall behind you, a little lower in pitch. Jump at the right moment to sail over most of them. If you're still on the ground when you step onto one, it will catch you. There are six exceptions: you crouch under projectiles, you switch off force fields with one of your hands, you scare animals away by clapping, you break walls with your weapons, you open doors with your hands, and you dodge barriers by switching lanes.
A jump lasts about a second. You can't jump again until you land, and when you land you'll hear the ground you came down on.
Runs are endless. The goal is to get as far as you can before something stops you.

Lanes.

The belt has three lanes, left, middle and right, and every run starts in the middle one. Press the left arrow or Q to move one lane to the left, and the right arrow or E to move one lane to the right. You can switch at any time, even in the middle of a jump or a crouch, except while a door is carrying you. You'll hear yourself turn from the lane you move into, and if you try to go past the left or right edge of the belt, you'll bump into it and stay where you are. Press L to hear which lane you're in.
Most obstacles fill the whole belt, so they always sound straight ahead of you whichever lane you're in, and switching lanes won't get you past them. Barriers and items are different: each one sits in a single lane. One in your lane sounds straight ahead, one a lane over sounds off to that side, and one two lanes over sounds further out still. Powerups fill the whole belt like obstacles, so you can collect one from any lane.
Sometimes two barriers or two items turn up side by side in different lanes. When two sound the same, one on each side of you, your ears can blend them into a single sound that seems to come from straight ahead, even though nothing is in your lane. There are two ways to tell. A barrier in your lane always plays its countdown, so a barrier that sounds centred but has no countdown means there's one on each side of you, and the lane you're in is the open one. For items, or whenever you're not sure, switch lanes for a moment: two side by side split apart straight away, one coming in front of you and the other going far off to the side.

Obstacles.

Hazards, such as pits, chasms, fire, lava, water, quicksand and electricity, end your run straight away if you fall into one. Each hazard has a five-step ledge leading up to it, and as you step along the ledge you'll hear a five-cue countdown, one cue per step, with the last cue on the step right before the hazard. At slower speeds, wait for the last few cues before you jump. Once you're fast enough, jumping on any ledge step will carry you over.
Spikes swing back and forth with their own rhythm, which you'll hear as you approach them. Getting hit by spikes takes 20 of your health.
Mines make a sound when they appear, then light up and start ticking once you get close. Stepping onto a mine takes 60 of your health. Once you jump over a mine, you'll hear it keep ticking as it fades out behind you.
Force fields hum ahead of you while they're on, and jumping or crouching won't get you past one. Press either hand key during a force field's countdown to switch it off. You'll hear the switch, then the field's sound changes as it shuts off, and you can run straight through. At the fastest speeds, where the countdown is very short, you can still switch one off in the last half second before you reach it. If you reach a force field while it's still on, it takes 30 of your health and throws you 5 steps forward past it.
Projectiles, such as missiles, fly toward you at head height, twice as fast as you run, so you'll hear them rushing in. Hold the crouch key so you're crouched when one reaches you, and it flies over you and fades away behind. A crouch only lasts a second and a half, so at slower speeds wait for the last few countdown cues before you crouch. Jumping won't help, and if one hits you, it takes 40 of your health.
Walls are glass windows standing in your way, and you'll hear them ahead of you as you get closer. Jumping or crouching won't get you past one, so swing or fire your weapon at it as it comes. Your weapons reach about 2 seconds ahead of you, so start early. Each hit cracks it a little more, and once it shatters you can run straight through. Walls get tougher the faster you run: early walls break with a few swings of the bat, while the fastest ones take a few hits from a strong weapon. If you crash into a wall that's still standing, it takes 50 of your health.
Animals, birds and dogs, call out ahead of you, and every one has its own call. Jumping, crouching or a single hand won't get rid of one: clap by pressing both hand keys together during an animal's countdown, and it runs off, so you can run straight on. If an animal reaches you, it runs into you and takes 10 of your health.
Barriers, such as cars, boulders and firewalls, each fill a single lane, and you'll hear them in their own lane as they come. Jumping, crouching, your hands and your weapons are no use against one: the only way past is to be in another lane when it reaches you. Each one turns up in a random lane, and sometimes two come side by side, but never in all three lanes, so there's always a lane open. If a barrier reaches you in its lane, it takes 80 of your health and carries on past you, fading away behind.
Doors mark your progress. Each time you get another 50 milliseconds faster, a real door comes up ahead of you. Real doors are locked, so you'll need the key that turns up earlier in each stretch. Once the door is within about 2 seconds of you, choose a key in your inventory and press U to unlock it, and you'll hear the lock give. Then press either hand key in the last few steps before the door to open it, and the door carries you through into the next stretch. If you try to open a door that's still locked, you'll hear it refuse. While it moves you, your footsteps go quiet and you can't jump, crouch, switch lanes, use your weapons or use your inventory, until it closes behind you. Once you reach the top speed, there are no more real doors.
Watch out for fake doors, though, which can turn up at any time. They sound slightly higher or lower than a real door, and they won't open: your hands or a key just find them jammed, and keys aren't used up on them. Break them with your weapons the same way as walls, but they're much tougher, about twice as tough as a wall at any speed, so draw your strongest weapon and start early. Doors, real and fake, have a countdown too, so you'll hear them coming. Crashing into any door, real or fake, takes 70 of your health.
Spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles, walls, animals, barriers and doors have the same five-cue countdown as hazards, at every speed, but without a ledge. A wall's countdown plays two notes at once, an animal's and a barrier's three, and a door's four. A barrier's countdown only plays while you're in its lane, so it stops as soon as you move out of the way and starts again if you move back in. Once a wall is broken, a force field switched off, an animal scared away, a real door opened or a fake one broken, its countdown stops. Each kind of obstacle has its own countdown sounds, so you can tell which one is coming from the countdown alone.

Health and lives.

You start each run with 1 life and 200 health. Spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles, walls, animals, barriers and doors each take some of your health when they catch you, and you'll hear yourself get hit. When your health runs out, you lose a life: if you have lives to spare, you'll hear a sound, your health fills back up to 200, and you keep running. If it was your last life, the run ends and you're asked whether you want to replay it. Hazards are different: falling into one ends your run straight away, however much health or how many lives you have. A steel shield takes those hits before your health does, health potions heal you a little, and life capsules give you more lives. Press H to hear how much health you have left, and how many lives.

Crouching.

You can crouch by holding down the crouch key. Your footsteps go quiet while you're crouched, and you stand back up when you let go, or on your own after a second and a half. After standing up, you have to wait half a second before you can crouch again. Pressing the jump key while crouched jumps straight away. Crouching is how you get under projectiles.

Weapons.

You have ten weapons, one on each number key from 1 to 0: the bat, boot, club, feat, fist, hammer, knife, laser, machine gun and pistol. Each run starts with the bat in your hands, and each weapon is stronger than the one before it. Press the space bar to swing a melee weapon or fire a gun. A melee weapon can swing up to five times a second and the pistol fire up to ten times a second, one swing or shot per press, while the machine gun keeps firing ten times a second for as long as you hold the space bar. A melee weapon hits straight away, while a gun fires a bullet that you'll hear flying off ahead of you until it lands. A bullet only flies as far as your weapons reach, so a shot fired at something still too far away falls short and wastes the round.
The machine gun and pistol use ammo. Each starts every run with 50 rounds loaded and 50 in reserve, and they keep their own ammo separately. A reserve can hold up to 200 rounds. Once a gun is empty you'll hear it click instead of firing. You can reload any time a gun isn't full, and you'll hear a ping when it's ready. When the reserve runs out, that gun is empty until you open an ammo pack.
Your weapons are for breaking walls and fake doors, and they all reach the same distance ahead of you. Real doors can't be harmed. The stronger the weapon, the fewer hits a wall takes. Your weapons always hit the nearest thing ahead of you, and that includes any item lying ahead of you in your lane, such as an ammo pack, key or shield, which breaks if you hit it. Items in other lanes are safe from your weapons.

Items and your inventory.

Every so often an ammo pack turns up ahead of you instead of an obstacle, in one of the three lanes, and you'll hear it waiting there. Get into its lane, then press either hand key in the last few steps before you reach it to pick it up, and it goes into your inventory. Be careful: if you run onto a pack in its lane, or land on it from a jump, it breaks and is lost, and so does shooting or swinging at it. A jump that carries you right over it just misses it, and a pack in another lane just passes you by. Sometimes two items turn up side by side in different lanes, so you have to choose which one to go for.
Packs stay in your inventory until you open them. Opening one adds 5 to 10 rounds to the reserve of the gun you're holding, so draw the gun you want to fill first. If you're holding a melee weapon, or that gun's reserve is already full, the pack stays in your inventory for later. Your inventory starts empty every run.
Keys turn up once in every stretch, somewhere before its real door, and you pick them up and lose them the same way as ammo packs. They come in several colors, but the color doesn't matter: any key unlocks any real door. Each key you use is gone, and a key you don't use stays in your inventory for a later door. If there's no door within reach, the door ahead is already unlocked, or it's a fake door, the key stays in your inventory.
Steel shields turn up now and then, a little less often than ammo packs, and you pick them up and lose them the same way. Choose one in your inventory and press U to wear it like armor. You can only wear one at a time, so any others wait in your inventory until the one you're wearing breaks. A shield takes the damage from spikes, mines, force fields, projectiles, walls, animals, barriers and doors in place of your health. You'll hear the obstacle hit your shield instead of you, and a force field still throws you forward. A fresh shield takes 400 damage, twice your own health: that's 40 animals, 20 spikes, 14 force fields, 10 projectiles, 8 walls, 7 mines, 6 door crashes or 5 barriers, and the hit that wears it out is still fully blocked, so you'll hear it break and lose nothing. Shields can't save you from hazards. Press M to hear how strong your shield is.
Life capsules turn up only rarely, and you pick them up and lose them the same way as ammo packs. Use one with U and you get 1 or 2 extra lives, up to a limit of 20 lives. You can carry as many capsules as you find, and if you're already at 20 lives, it tells you so and keeps the capsule for later.
Health potions turn up more often than life capsules, and you pick them up and lose them the same way. Use one with U to heal 5 to 10 percent of your health, never past full. If your health is already full, it tells you so and keeps the potion for later, so collecting a few is worth it.
Player teleporters are a gamble. Pick one up the same way, then press U, and it throws you a random 2 to 5 seconds' worth of belt either forward or back. It never drops you right on top of an obstacle, but it can drop you just in front of one, so be ready to react the moment you land. If it sends you back, anything you passed that's still waiting there, such as a spike or a hazard, comes up again, while anything you already dealt with, such as a broken wall or an opened door, stays that way.

Powerups.

Every so often a powerup turns up ahead of you. Unlike items, you don't pick it up with your hands, and it doesn't go into your inventory. A ground powerup sits on the belt, and you collect it by running onto it, so jumping over it skips it. A flight powerup floats above the belt, with its own sound repeating a little more slowly, and you collect it by being in the air as you reach it, so running underneath misses it. You can't tell a good powerup from a bad one until you collect it, so going for one is a gamble. Powerups don't sit in a lane, so you can collect one whichever lane you're in.
When you collect one, you hear it and its name is spoken. Its effect lasts 15 seconds, and you're told when it wears off. Powerups come in pairs, one good and one bad, and a new one replaces whichever of its pair is already running instead of adding to it, so you can have one effect of each pair going at once.
The pairs are: Slow Motion and Rush Hour, which make the belt slower or faster; Moon Jump and Short Hop, which make your jumps longer or shorter; Long Crouch and Stiff Knees, which make your crouches longer or shorter; Quick Swings and Clumsy Swings, which make your melee weapons swing faster or slower; Rapid Fire and Sticky Trigger, which make your guns fire faster or slower; Tough Hide and Soft Hide, which make hits do half or double damage to your health; and Sturdy Shield and Brittle Shield, which make hits do half or double damage to your shield. The shield pair only turns up while you're wearing a shield, and ends early if your shield breaks.

Menus.

The main menu has eight options. The documentation menu lets you read the changelog, credits, readme and todo list, each in a window you can move through with the arrow keys and close when you're done. The game menu is where you start a run, by choosing play game, or learn the game's sounds, as described below. The settings menu, change game version and check for updates are described below too. Test speakers plays a sound so you can check your speakers or headphones, and you can press enter to end it early. Restart game closes Stride Rush and opens it again, and exit game closes it. Pressing escape on the main menu also exits.
During a run, press P for the pause menu, where you can resume, restart or quit the run. Restarting or quitting asks you first, since you'll lose the run you're on. Pressing escape during a run asks straight away whether you want to quit, and the run stops moving while you decide. Whenever you leave a run this way, its sounds fade out.
When a run ends in a death, you're asked whether you want to replay the current run. Yes starts a new one straight away, and no takes you back to the game menu.

Learning the sounds.

The learn sounds menu, in the game menu, lets you play the game's sounds before you run into them. It has four categories: countdowns, with the countdown of each kind of obstacle; items, with common items and keys; traps, with a list for each kind of obstacle, and animals split into birds and dogs; and weapons, with artillery and melee. Each category tells you how many sounds it holds, and everything is in alphabetical order. Escape or back takes you up one level.
In a list of sounds, press enter to play the one you're on, or hold it down to keep starting it again. A sound with several versions plays one of them at random each time. Moving to another sound stops the one playing, space pauses or resumes it, and shift plus page up or page down makes the sounds louder or quieter. A countdown plays its five cues at the starting speed, and you can keep moving through the list while it plays. Each menu remembers where you were until you close the game.

Updates.

Stride Rush can update itself. When it starts, it checks quietly for a new version, and only speaks up if there is one; you can turn this off in the settings menu. Choose check for updates in the main menu to check at any time, and it tells you if you already have the latest version or if it can't check right now.
When there's a new version, you're asked whether to download it, and then whether you want the Windows installer or the portable version. While it downloads, you'll hear beeps that rise as it goes. Press space for the percentage, 1, 2 and 3 for the file size, how much has downloaded and how much is left, m to mute or unmute the beeps, h to hear these keys again, or escape to cancel. Afterwards, run installer starts the installer and closes the game, so the new version replaces this one. Extract file unpacks the portable version into an sdr folder inside your game folder, then closes the game, so you can run the new version from there.
Change game version, in the main menu, lets you type a different version number for the updater to compare against, using numbers and periods only. Type an older one and check for updates offers you the latest version again. It lasts until you close the game.

Settings.

The settings menu is a window with a list of tabs at the top. Move through the tabs with the arrow keys, and tab through the options on each one. Nothing you tick takes effect until you choose save settings, and cancel or escape leaves everything as it was.
General: reset settings puts every setting back to how it started, after asking you first. Your key bindings aren't touched.
Game: choose whether things behind you sound a little lower in pitch, which is ticked to start with and helps you tell what's behind you from what's ahead, whether the game checks for updates when it starts, which is also ticked to start with, and whether escape asks before quitting a run. If you untick the escape question, escape leaves the run straight away.
Keyboard: view every key binding, or rebind your keys. In the rebind list, you can add another key to an action, change an action's key, or remove an action's keys, and each change is saved as soon as you make it.
Menu: turn on wrapping, first letter navigation, position information and side scrolling in menus, and choose whether the learn sounds menu tells you when a sound has several versions, which is ticked to start with.
Sound: turn HRTF on or off, which is ticked to start with and makes sounds in front of you, behind you and to your sides easier to place through headphones. You can also choose between fading out by volume, which is ticked to start with, or by pitch when you leave a run, choose whether the sounds in the learn sounds menu keep looping until you move to another, which is also ticked to start with, and pick which sound device the game plays through. Your device choice is saved straight away.
Speech: set up your voice, either your screen reader or another voice with its own rate, pitch and volume, and preview how it sounds.
During a run, F2 opens the settings with only the game, menu and speech tabs, and F1 shows your key bindings. The run is paused while either is open, and carries on when you close it.
Your settings, including your music, ambience and menu music volumes, are remembered from one launch to the next.

Keyboard commands

Up arrow or letter W: Jumps, if pressed.
Down arrow or letter S: Crouches, to duck under projectiles, if held down.
Left arrow or letter Q: Moves one lane to the left, if pressed.
Right arrow or letter E: Moves one lane to the right, if pressed.
Tab or letter A: Uses your left hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item, if pressed.
Enter or letter D: Uses your right hand, to switch off a force field, open a door or pick up an item, if pressed.
Both hand keys together, such as A and D or Tab and Enter: Claps, to scare away an animal, if pressed. With nothing for your hands to do, a single hand makes a whoosh and a clap is just a clap.
Letter I: Moves to the next item in your inventory, if pressed.
Shift plus letter I: Moves to the previous item in your inventory, if pressed.
Letter U: Uses the item you're on in your inventory, such as opening an ammo pack, unlocking a door with a key, putting on a shield, using a life capsule, a health potion or a teleporter, if pressed.
Numbers 1 through 0: Draws the bat, boot, club, feat, fist, hammer, knife, laser, machine gun or pistol, if pressed.
Space: Swings or fires your weapon, if pressed. With the machine gun, it keeps firing, if held down.
Letter R: Reloads your gun, if pressed.
Letter X: Announces how much ammo your gun has loaded and in reserve, if pressed.
Letter H: Announces how much health you have left, as a percentage, and how many lives you have, if pressed.
Letter M: Announces how strong the shield you're wearing is, as a percentage, if pressed.
Letter L: Announces which lane you're in, if pressed.
Letter Z: Announces how far you've run, in tiles, if pressed.
Letter C: Announces your current speed, in milliseconds per step, if pressed.
Letter T: Announces how long you've been running for, if pressed.
Page up: Raises the in-game music volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Page down: Lowers the in-game music volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Home: Raises the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
End: Lowers the in-game ambience volume by 1 decibel, if pressed/held down.
Letter P: Brings up the pause menu, if pressed. The pause menu lets you resume, restart or quit the run. Pressing escape in the pause menu also resumes the run.
Escape: Asks whether you want to quit the run, or quits it straight away if you've turned the question off in the settings menu, if pressed.
F1: Shows every key binding, with the run paused until you close it, if pressed.
F2: Opens the settings menu with its game, menu and speech tabs, with the run paused until you close it, if pressed.
Alt plus F4: Exits the game right away during a run, if pressed.

Speech buffer commands

Everything the game speaks during a run is kept in speech buffers, so you can go back and review it. The all buffer holds every message, and the rest each hold one kind: doors, for locked, jammed and unlocked doors; items, for what you pick up, your inventory and what your items do; powerups, for the powerups you collect and when they wear off; status, for your health, shield, lane, distance, speed and running time; weapons, for drawing, reloading and your ammo; and misc, for your music and ambience volume. A buffer only shows up once something has been spoken into it, and they come in alphabetical order after all.
Comma: Moves to the previous message in the current buffer, if pressed/held down.
Period: Moves to the next message in the current buffer, if pressed/held down.
Shift plus comma: Moves to the first message in the current buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus period: Moves to the last message in the current buffer, if pressed.
Left bracket: Moves to the previous buffer, if pressed/held down.
Right bracket: Moves to the next buffer, if pressed/held down.
Shift plus left bracket: Moves to the first buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus right bracket: Moves to the last buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus letter M: Mutes or unmutes the current buffer, if pressed.
Shift plus letter C: Copies the current message to the clipboard, if pressed.
Alt plus shift plus letter C: Opens the current message in a box you can read with the arrow keys, if pressed.
Shift plus letter B: Brings up the buffer menu, where you can copy, export, mute or review a buffer or a message, if pressed.
Shift plus backslash: Exports the current buffer to a text file, then clears it, if pressed.
